\subsection{Every point but one, with an almost maximally far exception}
\label{sec:paired-domain}

Pairing coordinates as $x,-x$ gives a stronger separation for the
$c_1=c_2=1$ prescription. The exceptional point can be just one
coordinate short of the maximum possible Reed--Solomon distance,
while its separation from the nearby radius approaches the entire
capacity gap. The construction uses random paired domains and a
multiplicative-character estimate, without a matching-moment class.

\begin{lemma}[Sampling with blocks of paired coordinates]
\label{pd:sampling}
Let $r,m,D,q$ be positive integers with $D<m$ and
$m+rq\le(p-1)/2$, where $p>\max\{(4r+2)^2,2m^2\}$ is prime. Put
\[
 L=\binom mD,\qquad
 \delta=\frac{p^r}{L}+
 \frac{(4r+2)^{2r+2}+2m^2}{p-2m^2}.
\]
There is an expected $O(n^2)$-field-operation randomized procedure
outputting a length-$n=2(m+rq)$, dimension-$K=2D-1$ Reed--Solomon
code and a received line. Every output has maximum agreement exactly
$2D=K+1$ at parameter zero. For every $\gamma>0$, the probability
that some nonzero parameter has fewer than $2D+2r$ agreements is at most
\begin{equation}\label{pd:failure}
 \frac{q\delta}{\gamma}
 +(p-1)\left(\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\right)^q.
\end{equation}
Whenever all nonzero parameters are nearby, there is no correlated
agreement at that threshold.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $m+rq$ distinct orbits $\{a,-a\}$ uniformly from $\F_p^*$.
Use the first $m$ as the core and divide the remaining orbits into
$q$ blocks of $r$ orbits each. For every $D$-subset $I$ of the core,
write
\[
 H_I(X)=\prod_{i\in I}(X^2-a_i^2).
\]
Fix one reference support $I_0$, put $w=H_{I_0}$, and consider the
candidates $P_I=w-H_I$. Their leading terms cancel and all odd
coefficients vanish, so $\deg P_I\le2D-2<K$. Each candidate has
exactly $2D$ core agreements. On each padding pair choose a shared
uniform nonzero direction, independently across pairs. Set $f=w$
everywhere and $g=0$ on the core.

We bound the image of $I\mapsto(-H_I(x_1),\ldots,-H_I(x_r))$ for a
random outside block. Let $U=p-1$ and $V=U^r$. Fix representatives
$x_1,\ldots,x_r$ with distinct nonzero squares. Independently sampling
seed variables from
\[
 T=\F_p\setminus\{0,\pm x_1,\ldots,\pm x_r\}
\]
induces a probability measure $\mu$ on $(\F_p^*)^r$ by
$a\mapsto(x_j^2-a^2)_{j=1}^r$. Every nonprincipal Fourier coefficient
of $\mu$ has absolute value at most
\[
 \frac{2r\sqrt p+2r+1}{p-2r-1}
 \le \frac{4r+2}{\sqrt p}=:\lambda<1.
\]
Indeed, factor $x_j^2-a^2$ into the two distinct linear factors at
$\pm x_j$ and apply the product-character bound
\cite[Lemma 17]{bgks-shifted}, with constant additive phase. At least
one of the $2r$ characters is nonprincipal. The extra $2r+1$ allows
for the deleted arguments. The displayed hypotheses imply the second
inequality.

For two supports with $u$ indices on each side of their symmetric
difference, independent seeds give vector-collision probability
\[
 \frac1V\sum_{\chi}|\widehat\mu(\chi)|^{2u}
 \le \frac1V+\lambda^{2u}.
\]
Conditioning all $m$ seeds to have distinct squares costs at most
$1/a_0$, where $a_0=1-2m^2/p>0$, by the union bound.
After this conditioning, pairs with $1\le u\le r$ cannot collide:
cancel common factors and regard the residual products as distinct
monic degree-$u$ polynomials in $Y=X^2$. Their difference has degree
at most $u-1<r$, so cannot vanish at the $r$ distinct values $x_j^2$.
For $u\ge r+1$, the conditional collision probability is at most
$(V^{-1}+\lambda^{2r+2})/a_0$.

Let $E$ be the collision energy of the block image, counting ordered
support pairs, and set $\Delta=VE/L^2-1\ge0$. Counting the diagonal
pairs separately gives
\[
 \mathbb E\Delta\le\frac VL+
 \frac{1+V\lambda^{2r+2}}{a_0}-1\le\delta.
\]
This expectation applies to a uniform core and an outside block.
Equivalently one may choose the block first and the admissible core
second: each has the same number of compatible choices. Every block
in the sampled domain has this marginal law, so Markov's inequality
and a union bound give
$\Pr(\text{some block has }\Delta>\gamma)\le q\delta/\gamma$.
No independence among these events is assumed.

When $\Delta\le\gamma$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives an image of size at
least $V/(1+\gamma)$. Conditional on the core and blocks, the random
direction vectors are independent and uniform in $(\F_p^*)^r$.
For any fixed nonzero label $z$, multiplication of a direction vector
by $z$ preserves this distribution. Thus, if all blocks are good, the
expected number of uncovered labels is at most the second term of
\eqref{pd:failure}. A block hit supplies $2r$ padding agreements.
Combining the two failure events proves the bound.

The polynomial $w$ is monic of degree $2D$, and zero already agrees
at its $2D$ core roots. This proves the exact far distance for every
output and rules out correlated agreement. Sampling distinct orbits
and forming and evaluating $w$ take expected $O(n^2)$ field operations.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact rates and parity.}
The same construction realizes every fixed rational rate, with at most
one additional coordinate. Take $n$ through odd multiples of the reduced
denominator of $\rho$, and set $K=\rho n$. If $K$ is odd, use
$D=(K+1)/2$ and add zero if $n$ is odd, with $f(0)=w(0),g(0)=0$.
No selected candidate agrees there, since $H_I(0)\ne0$. If $K$ is
even, then $n$ is odd. Take $D=K/2$, multiply $w$ and all candidates
by $X$, and add zero with $f(0)=g(0)=0$. On a padding pair use
$x h,-x h$ as its directions. Dimension, threshold and exact far
agreement all increase by one. In both cases the nearby threshold is
$K+2r+1$, and the exact far agreement is $K+1$.
The probability bound is unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed separation arbitrarily close to the full gap]
\label{pd:punctured-line}
Fix rational $\rho\in(0,1)$, an integer $r\ge1$, $c_1>0$, and
$0<c_2<1+1/(2r)$. For every sufficiently large prime $p$, a code of
exact rate $\rho$ and length $n=\Theta(\log p)$ has a received line
with every nonzero parameter within radius $\theta=1-\rho-\eta$,
where $\eta=(2r+1)/n$, and parameter zero at distance
\[
 1-\rho-1/n=\theta+\frac{2r}{2r+1}\eta.
\]
The radius is strictly below Elias, there is no correlated agreement,
and $c_1n2^{c_2H_2(\rho)/\eta}=o(p)$. The code and line can be
sampled in expected polynomial time in $\log p$, with success
probability $1-p^{-\Omega(1)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\rho<\beta<1$ close to $\rho$, put $\alpha=\rho/\beta$,
and choose
\[
 \frac{2r}{\alpha H_2(\beta)}<C<
 \frac{2r+1}{\max\{1,c_2\}H_2(\rho)}.
\]
Take $n=C\log_2p+O(1)$ with the rate parity above,
$m=\alpha n/2+O(r)$, and $D=\rho n/2+O(1)$, adjusting $m$ so the
padding forms complete blocks. Then
$L=\binom mD\ge p^{r+\epsilon}$ for some fixed $\epsilon>0$,
and $q=\Theta(\log p)$. Hence $\delta=p^{-\Omega(1)}$.
A sufficiently small fixed $\gamma$ makes both terms of
\eqref{pd:failure} $p^{-\Omega(1)}$. The degree and threshold
identities give the distances. Finally
$\eta\log_2p\to(2r+1)/C>\max\{1,c_2\}H_2(\rho)$ proves strict
Elias and the numerical comparison.
\end{proof}
