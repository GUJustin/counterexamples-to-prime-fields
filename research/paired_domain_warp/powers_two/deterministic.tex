\subsubsection{A deterministic family with unique and recoverable witnesses}
\label{sec:paired-powers-two}

The unambiguous construction can be made deterministic, with an efficient
procedure deciding whether a parameter is nearby and recovering its unique
witness. Its core representatives are simply powers of two.

\begin{lemma}[Decoding products of $4^i-1$]\label{pd:integer-products}
For distinct subsets $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$, the positive integers
$T_I=\prod_{i\in I}(4^i-1)$ are distinct. Given $T_I$, its support can
be recovered using a polynomial number of bit operations in $m$ and
$\log T_I$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $s=\sum_{i\in I}i$ and
$U_I=\prod_{i\in I}(1-4^{-i})$, so $T_I=4^sU_I$. The elementary
product inequality gives $2/3<U_I\le1$. For a nonempty support,
$T_I$ therefore has exactly $2s$ binary digits, determining $s$ and
hence the rational number $U_I$.

Process indices in increasing order, removing previously selected factors
from $U_I$. At index $i$, the remaining product is at most $1-4^{-i}$
if $i$ is selected. If $i$ is not selected, it is at least
\[
 \prod_{j=i+1}^{m}(1-4^{-j})
 \ge1-\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}4^{-j}
 >1-\tfrac13 4^{-i}>1-4^{-i}.
\]
These comparisons determine every membership bit, proving injectivity.
They can be implemented with integers: if the remaining integer product
is $t$ and the remaining index sum is $s$, compare
$t4^i$ with $(4^i-1)4^s$. On selecting $i$, divide $t$ by $4^i-1$ and
subtract $i$ from $s$. For an arbitrary input, require exact divisibility
at every selection and verify the final product and support size.
All intermediate integers have polynomial bit length.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic, efficiently decoded counterexamples]
\label{pd:deterministic}
Let $1\le D<m$, let $p$ be prime with
\[
 p>2^{D(2m-D+1)},
\]
and use core representatives $a_i=2^i$ for $1\le i\le m$, with padding
$\pm1$ and the exact-rate parity convention above. At threshold $K+3$,
the resulting line has exactly $\binom mD$ nearby parameters, each with
a unique nearby codeword. Parameter zero has distance $1-K/n-1/n$,
and each nearby parameter has distance $1-K/n-3/n$. The code, line,
and a procedure deciding membership in the nearby set and recovering
the unique witness are deterministic and polynomial in $\log p$.

Consequently, for every fixed rational rate $\rho\in(0,1)$ and every
$c_1>0$, $0<c_2<3/2$, all sufficiently large prime fields have such
examples at exact rate $\rho$ and length $n=\Theta_\rho(\sqrt{\log p})$,
strictly below Elias, with no correlated agreement and
\[
 \frac{\binom mD}{c_1n2^{c_2H_2(\rho)/(3/n)}}=2^{\Omega_{\rho,c_2}(n)}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every positive $D$-subset product is less than
\[
 \prod_{i=m-D+1}^m4^i=2^{D(2m-D+1)}<p.
\]
The displayed field bound also gives $p>2^{m+1}$. No nonzero ternary
sum of $2,4,\ldots,2^m$ can vanish modulo $p$: its integer absolute
value is less than $p$, and its largest power exceeds the sum of all
smaller powers. Thus the signed-relation hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{pd:unique-criterion} holds. The nearby labels are
\[
 z_I=(-1)^{D+1}T_I\pmod p.
\]
Lemma~\ref{pd:integer-products} and the no-wrap bound show that these
labels are distinct. They also give a decoder: lift
$(-1)^{D+1}z$ to $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$, recover and check its support,
then output the reference locator minus that support's locator, with
the optional factor $X$. The exact list classification guarantees
that rejection means the parameter is outside the tested radius.
The degree and distance assertions follow from the same criterion.

For exact rate $\rho$, choose the admissible $n$ near a sufficiently
small constant multiple of $\sqrt{\log_2p}$. Then
$m=n/2+O(1)$, $D=\rho n/2+O_\rho(1)$, and the displayed field bound
holds. As before,
$\log_2\binom mD=nH_2(\rho)/2+O_\rho(\log n)$, which proves the
numerical separation for $c_2<3/2$. Moreover
$(3/n)\log_2p\to\infty$, proving strict Elias. The far point rules
out correlated agreement. Forming the locators, evaluating them, and
performing the integer comparisons use polynomial time in $\log p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[An exact two-level distance profile]
\label{pd:powers-two-level}
In the unadjusted case $n=2m+2$, $K=2D-1$, assume additionally
\[
 p>2^{m(m+1)/2+2m+2}.
\]
Every parameter outside the nearby bank has distance exactly
$(n-K-1)/n$. Thus the line has only two distance levels, and the
integer decoder determines which level holds. The three half-rate
rows in the preceding table satisfy this extra bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The zero codeword supplies $K+1$ reference-core agreements at every
parameter, so distance is always at most $(n-K-1)/n$. Suppose a codeword
has at least $K+2$ agreements. At least one padding position must agree,
by the root bound. If exactly one padding position agrees,
its $K+1$ core roots have sum zero; integer dissociation forces them
into pairs. Its locator is even, so the other padding position agrees
too, placing the parameter in the nearby bank.

It remains to consider two padding agreements and only $K$ known core
roots. The difference polynomial is monic of degree $K+1$, with zero
coefficient of $X^K$. Its last root is therefore minus the sum of those
$K$ roots. Cancel complete sign-pairs among the known roots and call
the remaining signed powers of two $y_1,\ldots,y_u$. Their number is
odd. If $u=1$, the last root completes its pair, again giving a bank
locator. For $u\ge3$, put $S=\sum_i y_i$. Equality of the two padding
values would require
\[
 F=(1+S)\prod_i(1-y_i)-(1-S)\prod_i(1+y_i)=0\pmod p.
\]
This integer is nonzero. Indeed, writing $e_j$ for the elementary
symmetric functions of the $y_i$ gives
\[
 F/2=(Se_2-e_3)+(Se_4-e_5)+\cdots.
\]
If the two smallest-magnitude signed powers are $u=\pm2^a$ and
$v=\pm2^b$, with $a<b$, the monomial with uniquely smallest $2$-adic
valuation is $u^2v$ in the first parenthesis, with valuation $2a+b$.
All other degree-three terms and all higher-degree terms have larger
valuation. Thus cancellation is impossible.
Finally,
\[
 |F|\le2(1+|S|)\prod_i(1+|y_i|)
 <2^{m(m+1)/2+2m+2}<p,
\]
so $F$ cannot vanish modulo $p$ either. Consequently every improvement
over the far distance is already a nearby-bank point.
\end{proof}
