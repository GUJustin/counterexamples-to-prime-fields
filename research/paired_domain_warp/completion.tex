\subsubsection{Completing the joint image and determining exact distances}

A few additional core choices can fill the entire product image. This
removes the need for multiple padding blocks and gives an exact distance
profile on an affine space. The completion step follows the classical
random-translate strategy of Erd\H{o}s and R\'enyi~\cite[Theorem 2]{erdos-renyi-group}; here a character bound controls
nonuniform translates arising from new evaluation points.

\begin{lemma}[Completion by pairs of alternatives]\label{pd:completion}
Fix $r$ nonzero padding representatives with distinct squares. Let
$m_0,D_0,t$ be positive integers with $D_0<m_0$, put
$n=2(m_0+2t+r)$ and $C=4r+2$, and assume
$p>\max\{(n+1)^2,C^2\}$. Define
\[
 L_0=\binom{m_0}{D_0},\qquad
 d_0=\frac{p^r}{L_0}+
 \frac{C^{2r+2}+2m_0^2}{p-2m_0^2},\qquad
 \Phi(h)=\frac{h^2+C^2h/p}{1-4/p}.
\]
If $(p-1)^r\Phi^{\circ t}(d_0)<1$, there is a core of $m_0+2t$
sign-orbits and a family of $(D_0+t)$-subsets whose locator values on
the padding fill $(\F_p^*)^r$.
More generally, uniform successive sampling of the core succeeds with
failure probability at most
\begin{equation}\label{pd:completion-failure}
 \frac{d_0}{h_0}+\sum_{i=1}^t\frac{\Phi(h_{i-1})}{h_i}
\end{equation}
for any positive thresholds $h_0,\ldots,h_t$ with $(p-1)^rh_t<1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $m_0$ base orbits uniformly outside the padding. The energy
argument in Lemma~\ref{pd:sampling} applies with the padding fixed:
the mean missing fraction of the product image of all $D_0$-subsets
is at most $d_0$.

At each subsequent step add two new core orbits, represented by $a,b$.
Each candidate chooses exactly one of these two. If $S$ is the current
product image and $v(a)=(x_j^2-a^2)_{j=1}^r$, the new image is
$Sv(a)\cup Sv(b)$. For its complement $B$ of density $h$, the new
missing set is the intersection of two translates of $B$.

First sample $a,b$ independently outside zero, the padding, and all
previous core orbits. At most $n+1<\sqrt p$ field elements are excluded.
The same character estimate gives every nonprincipal Fourier coefficient
of the distribution $\mu$ of $v(a)$ absolute value at most $C/\sqrt p$.
Using normalized Fourier coefficients on $(\F_p^*)^r$, Parseval gives
\[
 \mathbb E\frac{|Bv(a)\cap Bv(b)|}{(p-1)^r}
 =\sum_\chi |\widehat{1_B}(\chi)|^2|\widehat\mu(\chi)|^2
 \le h^2+\frac{C^2}{p}h.
\]
The available set has size at least $p/2$. Conditioning $a\ne\pm b$
therefore costs at most $1/(1-4/p)$, so the conditional mean missing
fraction is at most $\Phi(h)$. This bound holds uniformly over every
previous choice of the core and its image.

Successive averaging proves the existence assertion: at each step
choose a pair attaining at most the conditional mean. Once the missing
fraction is less than $1/(p-1)^r$, no vector is missing. Alternatively,
Markov's inequality at the base step and conditionally at each later
step, followed by a union bound, gives \eqref{pd:completion-failure}.
The sampler does not need to compute any of the images.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact distances and almost the entire capacity gap]
\label{pd:full-gap}
Fix rational $\rho\in(0,1)$, put $b=\log_2p$ and $H=H_2(\rho)$,
and let $r=r(p)\ge1$ satisfy $r\log b=o(b)$. Over every sufficiently
large prime field, a code of exact rate $\rho$ and length
\[
 n=\frac{(2r+4/5)b}{H}+O_\rho(1)
\]
has an affine space with independent directions $e_1,\ldots,e_r$,
each supported on a disjoint pair of coordinates, such that
\begin{equation}\label{pd:exact-profile}
 \dist\!\left(f+\sum_{j=1}^r z_je_j,C\right)
 =1-\rho-\frac{1+2\operatorname{wt}(z)}n
 \qquad(z\in\F_p^r).
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{wt}(z)$ counts its nonzero entries. At radius
$\theta=1-\rho-\eta$, $\eta=(2r+1)/n$, the nearby parameters are
exactly $(\F_p^*)^r$, and their decoding lists are pairwise disjoint.
The radius is strictly below Elias, $n2^{H/\eta}=o(p)$, and there is
no correlated agreement. The code and affine space can be sampled in
expected polynomial time in $\log p$, with success probability
$1-p^{-\Omega(1)}$.

In particular, every line through $f$ with all direction coefficients
nonzero is nearby at all $p-1$ nonzero parameters and has direction
weight exactly $2r$. Its exceptional point has distance $1-\rho-1/n$,
only one coordinate short of maximum distance. If $r\to\infty$, the
separation is $(1-o(1))\eta$ and $\eta\sim H/b$.
Taking $r=\lfloor\log_2b\rfloor$ gives length $\Theta_\rho(b\log b)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Take $t=16(r+1)$ and choose $n$ as displayed with the exact-rate parity
specified above. Let $a\in\{0,1\}$ indicate the extra zero coordinate,
and let $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$ indicate multiplication by $X$. Set
\[
 m_0=(n-a)/2-2t-r,\qquad
 D_0=(\rho n+1-\epsilon)/2-t.
\]
These are positive with $D_0<m_0$ eventually. Since
$m_0=n/2-O(r)$ and $D_0=\rho n/2-O(r)$, the binomial entropy estimate
and smoothness of $H_2$ near the fixed rate give
\[
 \log_2 L_0=\frac{nH}{2}+O_\rho(r+\log n)
 =rb+2b/5+o(b).
\]
Thus $L_0\ge p^{r+1/3}$ eventually, while
$C^{2r+2}+m_0^2=p^{o(1)}$. The initial mean missing fraction is at most
$2p^{-1/3}$. With probability at least $1-2p^{-5/24}$, it is at most
$p^{-1/8}$. Whenever $h\le p^{-1/8}$, for sufficiently large $p$,
\[
 \Phi(h)\le4p^{-1/8}h.
\]
Consequently the probability that a step fails to reduce the missing
fraction by a factor $p^{-1/16}$ is at most $4p^{-1/16}$.
A zero missing fraction remains zero. A union bound over $t$ steps
gives failure probability at most
$2p^{-5/24}+4tp^{-1/16}=p^{-\Omega(1)}$; on success the final missing
fraction is at most $p^{-1/8-t/16}<p^{-r}$, hence is zero.

Fix a reference locator $w$ in the completed family. Its degree is
$K+1$ after the parity adjustment, and zero attains its $K+1$ roots,
so $\dist(f,C)=(n-K-1)/n$. The full product image supplies a candidate
matching any prescribed nonzero shifts on any subset of padding pairs:
extend the unspecified components to arbitrary nonzero values. If
$z$ has weight $j$, this gives $K+1+2j$ agreements. Conversely the
received word differs from $f$ on exactly $2j$ positions, so the
Hamming-distance inequality gives the matching lower bound. This
proves \eqref{pd:exact-profile}.

A codeword within $\theta$ at a full-support parameter must agree on
all $2r$ padding positions: otherwise changing those positions back to
$f$ would give a distance smaller than its established minimum.
Distinct full-support parameters have different padding values, so
their lists are disjoint. The far point rules out correlated agreement.

Finally $\eta b-H=\Theta_\rho(1/r)$ dominates the
$O_\rho(1/b)$ entropy-radius change, proving strict Elias, and
\[
 \log_2\left(\frac{n2^{H/\eta}}p\right)
 =\log_2n-\frac{b}{5(2r+1)}+O_\rho(1/r)\longrightarrow-\infty.
\]
The construction samples $O(n)$ field elements and forms and evaluates
one locator in $O(n^2)$ field operations. It does not recover a nearby
witness for an arbitrary supplied parameter.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Witness variation and extension-field challenges]
\label{pd:witness-extension}
For a completed line above, a polynomial codeword graph of parameter
 degree $e\ge1$ supplies nearby witnesses at at most
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{e(n-K-1)}{2r}\right\rfloor
\]
parameters. Consequently a polynomial graph selecting a nearby codeword
at every nonzero parameter has degree at least
$\lceil 2r(p-1)/(n-K-1)\rceil=\Omega_\rho(p/\log p)$.

Let $E/\F_p$ be any field extension and extend the Reed--Solomon code's
coefficient field to $E$. In the same affine space, at the same radius,
the nearby parameter vectors are exactly $(\F_p^*)^r\subseteq E^r$,
and their decoding lists are unchanged. In particular a completed line
has exactly the $p-1$ nonzero base-field nearby parameters over $E$.
Every parameter in $E\setminus\F_p$ has the same distance as the
exceptional point, namely $1-\rho-1/n$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:ct-quantitative-concurrency} with $a_0=K+1$ and
$T=K+2r+1$. If a graph supplies a nearby codeword at several parameters,
choose those codewords as the selected witnesses in that lemma. The
asymptotic degree bound follows from $n=\Theta_\rho(r\log p)$.

The distance of $f$ over $E$ is still $(n-K-1)/n$, by the same root
bound. To reach the tested radius after changing at most $2r$ padding
positions, every one must change and must match the nearby codeword.
That codeword also agrees with $f$ on at least $K+1$ core positions.
Interpolation on any $K$ of them forces its coefficients into $\F_p$,
since both the evaluation points and the values of $f$ lie in $\F_p$.
The padding equalities then force all parameter coordinates into
$\F_p^*$. Conversely every such vector already has a base-field nearby
codeword, and the same interpolation argument excludes additional
extension-field codewords from its list.

Finally fix a line parameter $z\notin\F_p$. There is an
$\F_p$-linear functional $\lambda:E\to\F_p$ with
$\lambda(1)=1$ and $\lambda(z)=0$. Applying it coefficientwise
to an extension-field codeword shows that every agreement with
$f+zg$ is an agreement of a base-field codeword with $f$. There are
at most $K+1$ such agreements. The zero codeword attains $K+1$ on
the reference core roots (and the optional common zero coordinate),
since $g$ vanishes there. This proves the exact distance.
\end{proof}

The polynomial-degree conclusion concerns algebraic witness selection;
it is not a lower bound on decoding running time. The extension-field
statement also limits the scope of the example: over a finite extension,
the nearby fraction on the line is $(p-1)/|E|$, not $1-1/|E|$.
