# powers of bounded-degree polynomials have linear MCA

September 16, 2026. Self-reviewed proof with exact finite checks. No
independent mathematical review or novelty claim. This excludes a
particular construction route; it does not prove a quadratic lower bound.

## Theorem and definitions

Fix r>=1. Candidates have the form

    P=c H^e,   c!=0, H monic of degree r, D=re<n.

Let the evaluation domain consist of n distinct field elements, and let
f,g be arbitrary words on it. For a label z and candidate P, use its
**full** agreement set S(P,z)={x:f(x)+z*g(x)=P(x)}. The pair (z,P) is
bad when |S(P,z)|>=A and no degree-at-most-D polynomials F0,G0 agree
with f,g respectively on all of S(P,z). Equivalently, g has no such
interpolant on this set, since F0=P-z*G0. A bad label has at least one
bad candidate. This definition requires A>D.

Fix constants 0<rho_min<=rho_max<a<=1 and r>=1. For
rho_min<=D/n<=rho_max, A>=a*n, and p/n tending to infinity
(or in characteristic zero), the number of bad labels is O_r,a(n).
More precisely, a fixed sufficiently large lower bound on p/n, depending
only on r,a, suffices once n is sufficiently large (depending also on
rho_min). In particular r=2
allows two independently moving roots, beyond the earlier one-parameter
MCA lemma. Here e is fixed within each candidate family, though it grows
with n. Arbitrarily varying root exponents are not covered.

The argument below gives explicit sufficient finite characteristic and
exponent thresholds, but very poor constants. It is not a practical
better.codes certificate.

## 1. A uniform gcd bound for high powers

Let t be 3 or 4. Let H_1,...,H_t be monic degree-r polynomials over an
algebraically closed field. Assume H_2/H_1,...,H_t/H_1 are multiplicatively
independent modulo constants. Equivalently, the integer divisor vectors
of the H_i are affinely independent over Q.

Take independent homogeneous linear forms F,G in t variables, and put
S=the union of the roots of all H_i, so |S|<=tr. For m>=1 set

    N=binom(m+t-1,t-1),
    J=binom(m+t-3,t-3),
    M=N-J.

Thus J=1 for t=3, and J=m+1 for t=4. Assume

    e > binom(N-1,2)*(tr-1),
    characteristic zero, or p>r*m*e.                     (A)

The common zeros outside S of F(H_1^e,...,H_t^e) and
G(H_1^e,...,H_t^e), counted with their common multiplicities, have total
at most

    K_t(m,r,e) = (t-1)*J*r*m*e/M + (tr-1)*(M-1)/2.        (B)

The leading coefficient, expressed as a multiple of D=re, tends to zero:
it is 4/(m+3) for t=3 and 18/(m+5) for t=4.

### 1.1 Independence of the degree-m monomials

The N homogeneous degree-m monomials in H_1^e,...,H_t^e are polynomials
of the same degree rme and have roots only in S. Suppose that a minimal
constant linear relation has q terms. The multiplicative independence
assumption rules out q=2. Any q-1 terms are linearly independent, so their
ordinary Wronskian is nonzero under p>rme. One proof of this familiar
fact uses a constant change of basis to distinct leading degrees, all
less than p; the resulting leading Wronskian coefficient is a nonzero
Vandermonde product in those degrees.

At a root s, omit a term having the smallest vanishing order from the
Wronskian. A minimal relation makes all these (q-1)-term Wronskians
constant multiples of one another. Its order is therefore at least

    sum_i ord_s(f_i) - min_i ord_s(f_i) - binom(q-1,2).

Let A be the common gcd of the q polynomials. Summing over S and comparing
with the degree upper bound (q-1)rme-binom(q-1,2) gives

    rme-deg A <= binom(q-1,2)*(|S|-1).

But rme-deg A is at least the rational degree of any ratio f_i/f_j.
Such a ratio is the e-th power of a nonconstant rational function, by
multiplicative independence, and thus has degree at least e. This
contradicts (A). Hence all N monomials are linearly independent.

### 1.2 Use the homogeneous ideal generated by F,G

Let V be the degree-m part of the ideal (F,G). Its dimension is M:
the quotient is a polynomial ring in t-2 variables, whose degree-m
part has dimension J. Evaluation at (H_1^e,...,H_t^e) is injective by
Section 1.1. Choose a basis psi_1,...,psi_M of the evaluated space and
let W be its nonzero Wronskian. Every psi_i has degree at most rme, so

    deg W <= M*rme-binom(M,2).                            (C)

At any s in S, write a_alpha for the orders of the N evaluated monomials.
A codimension-J subspace has a basis whose sum of orders is at least

    sum_alpha a_alpha - J*max_alpha a_alpha.

To see this without a genericity assumption, intersect V with the span
of monomials of order at least k for each integer k. Its dimension loses
at most J from that span. The resulting filtration dimensions give the
stated sum-of-orders bound. Cancellations can only increase orders.

Changing the basis by constants changes W by a nonzero scalar. Therefore

    ord_s W >= sum_alpha a_alpha-J*max_alpha a_alpha-binom(M,2).

Over all s in S, the first sum is exactly N*rme. Also

    sum_s max_alpha a_alpha
       = e*m*sum_s max_i ord_s H_i <= t*r*m*e.

Consequently

    sum_(s in S) ord_s W >= N*rme-J*t*rme-|S|*binom(M,2). (D)

Let T be the gcd of the two evaluated linear forms after removing its
factors supported on S. Every psi_i is divisible by T. The identity
Wr(T*f_1,...,T*f_M)=T^M Wr(f_1,...,f_M) gives the additional lower
bound M*deg T on zeros of W outside S. Combining (C) and (D), and
using N-M=J, proves (B).

This is an elementary specialization of the general high-power gcd
phenomenon. A broader characteristic-zero result is Guo--Sun--Wang,
Theorem 11, https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01896. Its characteristic-zero
theorem is not being silently applied to finite fields: the finite
characteristic condition needed here is proved in the Wronskian argument.

## 2. Few dependent tuples when each direction occurs boundedly often

Suppose a collection of L labeled candidates c_i H_i^e contains at most
T candidates with the same monic H. Let

    C_(t,r)=sum_(j=1)^(t-1) binom((j+1)r-1,r).

At most T*C_(t,r)*L^(t-1) ordered t-tuples have affinely dependent
divisor vectors. Indeed, at the first dependent entry, its roots lie in
the union of the preceding j root sets, containing at most jr points.
There are at most binom((j+1)r-1,r) monic degree-r polynomials supported
on that union. Each occurs at most T times. Count the other entries
freely and sum over j. This also covers repeated directions.

## 3. Constant fixed-word lists

Suppose the candidates agree with one fixed word on at least A>=a*n
positions. A fixed monic H supports at most

    T <= floor(n/(A-r))

distinct scalar multiples: after deleting its at most r roots, the
agreement positions for distinct scalars are disjoint.

For an independent triple the common agreement positions outside the
at most 3r roots are common zeros of the independent linear forms
c_1 Z_1-c_2 Z_2 and c_1 Z_1-c_3 Z_3. Thus they number at most

    K=K_3(m,r,e)+3r.

Choose m, then sufficiently large n,e, so that K<=a^3*n/16 and the conditions (A)
holds. This is possible uniformly at fixed positive rate when p/n tends
to infinity. If L>=4/a, counting ordered triples through coordinates gives

    sum_x (b_x)_3 >= n*(aL-2)^3 >= a^3*n*L^3/8.

Here b_x counts agreeing candidates. The first inequality follows from
(b)_3>=max(b-2,0)^3 and convexity, using sum_x b_x>=a*n*L.

Independent triples contribute at most K L^3; dependent triples
contribute at most T*C_(3,r)*n*L^2. Therefore

    L <= 16*T*C_(3,r)/a^3.

Together with the small-L case and T<=2/a for large n, this is a constant
list bound depending only on r,a.

## 4. Heavy ratio groups in the received-line problem

Now select one bad full-MCA candidate P=cH^e at each bad label z, with
full agreement support of size at least A. Let B=floor(A/2). Delete
the at most r roots of H from the support and group the remaining
positions by lambda_x=g(x)/H(x)^e.

If a group has at least B positions and its ratio is zero, P is in the
fixed-word list of f at threshold B. Each such fixed P gives at most n
bad labels: any bad support must contain a position where (f,g)!=(P,0),
and each such position determines at most one label.

If a heavy group's ratio is nonzero, lambda H^e belongs to the fixed-word
list of g at threshold B. For each such direction H, divide by H^e at
the nonroot coordinates. These coordinates become affine functions of z.
Except at at most r accidental labels on the removed roots, a bad
support must contain two distinct affine functions. A support of size
at least A-r then consumes at least A-r-1 pairs of distinct affine
functions, each pair meeting at at most one label. This gives at most

    r + floor(binom(n,2)/(A-r-1))

bad labels per heavy direction. If all the functions on a support were
identical, their two coefficients times H^e would be common witnesses.

Section 3 bounds both heavy lists by constants, so these contributions
are O(n).

## 5. The light directions have only constantly many labels altogether

In a light support every ratio group has fewer than B positions. After
removing roots, at least (A-r)(A-r-B+1)/2 coordinate pairs have distinct
ratios. For a fixed H each such pair determines at most one pair (z,c).
Thus the number of selected light labels in one direction is at most

    T=floor(n(n-1)/((A-r)(A-r-B+1))).

This is a constant at fixed positive A/n. Four candidates at four
distinct labels have, at each common agreement point, the relations

    (z2-z3)c1 H1^e+(z3-z1)c2 H2^e+(z1-z2)c3 H3^e=0,
    (z2-z4)c1 H1^e+(z4-z1)c2 H2^e+(z1-z2)c4 H4^e=0.

These are independent linear forms in the four powers. For an affinely
independent quadruple, Section 1 bounds its common support by
K_4(m,r,e)+4r. Choose m and then n,e,p so this is at most a^4*n/32.

If L>=6/a, ordered quadruple incidence is at least

    sum_x (b_x)_4 >= n*(aL-3)^4 >= a^4*n*L^4/16.

Section 2 and the independent-tuple bound give

    a^4*n*L^4/16 <= (a^4*n/32)*L^4 + T*C_(4,r)*n*L^3,

so L<=32*T*C_(4,r)/a^4. The light labels therefore contribute only a
constant. Combining with the heavy labels proves the proposed O(n)
conclusion. The zero polynomial contributes at most n extra bad labels.
The same pair argument bounds all constant candidates by O_a(n), so a
finite union over base degrees 0,...,r is covered too (with the ambient
degree cap unchanged).

## 6. Explicit order of parameter choices

Put b=a/3. Choose integers m3,m4 such that the coefficients of D in
K_3 and K_4 are respectively at most b^3/32 and a^4/64. For example m3>=128/b^3-3 and m4>=1152/a^4-5 suffice. Let beta_t be the constant term
(tr-1)(M_t-1)/2 in K_t. Require

    e > max_t binom(N_t-1,2)*(tr-1),
    p > D*max(m3,m4)             (omit in characteristic zero),
    beta_3+3r <= b^3*n/32,
    beta_4+4r <= a^4*n/64,
    n >= 6*(r+1)/a,  A>=3.

Since D<n, these ensure K_3+3r<=b^3*n/16 and
K_4+4r<=a^4*n/32. Also B=floor(A/2)>=A/3>=b*n,
so the triple argument applies to both heavy fixed-word lists at B.
Their scalar multiplicity is at most 2/b for these thresholds. Each list
therefore has size at most

    L0=max(ceil(4/b),ceil(32*C_(3,r)/b^4)).

The light-direction multiplicity is at most 8/a^2, and the total number
of selected light labels is at most

    L1=max(ceil(6/a),ceil(256*C_(4,r)/a^6)).

Consequently the nonzero degree-re family has at most

    L0*n + L0*(r+floor(binom(n,2)/(A-r-1))) + L1

bad labels. The zero polynomial adds at most n. The denominator is
positive under the thresholds above; the displayed bound is O_r,a(n).
The lower rate bound makes e grow linearly with n, so all exponent and
length thresholds eventually hold. Taking p/n sufficiently large implies
the stated characteristic inequality. No uniform claim is made as a
tends to zero or r grows with n.

## 7. Verification and limitations

`verify_wronskian.py`: seven exact fixtures check monomial
independence, ideal dimensions, valuation filtrations, Wronskian degree
and root-order accounting, and gcds away from the root union. Shared
roots and doubled roots are included. Frobenius and multiplicative-cycle
negative controls demonstrate why the hypotheses matter. The largest
polynomial degree is 1012. Small m makes these finite gcd bounds weak;
these tests do not numerically realize the asymptotic MCA constants.

`verify_mca.py`: 24 received lines give 73 bad witnesses,
including 23 light witnesses. Full-support interpolation, both heavy
cases, scalar multiplicities, and coordinate-pair counts pass. Separate
exhaustive divisor-dependence counts include all 49 monic quadratics over
F_7, and 25 directions each repeated twice; they include nonsplit and
repeated roots. These check the finite ingredients, not the entire
asymptotic theorem by enumeration.

The proof has been self-reviewed twice. No independent mathematical
review is claimed. It closes fixed powers of bounded-degree bases in
large characteristic, including the order-three quadratic-power family.
Variable root exponents, a growing number of distinct roots, general
nonlinear higher-order families, and the fixed-gap quadratic lower-bound
target remain open here.
