# Prime-power obstruction and ramified reduction to a smaller cyclic source

This extends the independently audited prime-order integrality argument. It concerns characteristic-zero candidates for the fixed word

`W_r(X)=(1+X^(2r))/2−X^r` on μ_(4r), of degree strictly below r.

It does not assume that a selected candidate is nearest, and does not infer that its residue candidate has a large orbit without an additional hypothesis.

## General source-reduction theorem

Write `r=q*s`, where `q=ell^a`, `a≥1`, ell is an odd prime, and `gcd(ell,s)=1`. Suppose a characteristic-zero polynomial P of degree below r agrees with W_r at M coordinates and

`M > r + (4s−2)q/ell`.                                      (1)

Then, over the finite field generated by μ_(4s) in characteristic ell, there is a polynomial P_a of degree below s agreeing with W_s on at least

`ceil(M/q)`                                                (2)

distinct coordinates of μ_(4s). In particular the agreement margin over dimension, divided by domain length, does not decrease:

`(ceil(M/q)−s)/(4s) ≥ (M−r)/(4r)`.

If ell≡1 modulo4s, both the domain and the candidate lie in F_ell. Otherwise the theorem gives a source over F_(ell^f), with f the multiplicative order of ell modulo4s, and does not assert a prime-field source.

### Integrality before reduction

Let A_S be the monic locator of the M agreeing roots. If M>2r there is already a contradiction from degree, so assume M≤2r. The monic polynomial

`F=X^(2r)−2X^r+1−2P`

is divisible by A_S, with quotient degree k=2r−M<r. Its quotient coefficients are determined successively from the coefficients of F above degree r, all zero except the leading1. Equivalently they are h₀,…,h_k of the selected roots. They are cyclotomic integers. Hence F=A_S Q is integral and P is integral after inverting2. This avoids any assumption that the ramifying Vandermonde denominators are units.

Reduce at a prime over ell in Q(ζ_(4r)). The reduction of μ_(4r) is μ_(4s), with exactly q original roots above each of its4s distinct points. Let n_c be the numbers of selected roots above each occupied residue point c. Then

`0<n_c≤q`, `sum n_c=M`, and the number t of occupied points is at most4s.

The reduced F has root multiplicity at least n_c at c.

### Iterative derivative argument with exact rounding

Work in the residue field, which is perfect. Inductively suppose

`F_bar=F_j^(ell^j)`,

where

`F_j=X^(2r/ell^j)−2X^(r/ell^j)+1−2P_j`,  `deg P_j<r/ell^j`.

For j=0 this is the reduction just constructed. Each occupied c has multiplicity at least

`m_(c,j)=ceil(n_c/ell^j)`

in F_j. For j<a, the exponent r/ell^j is divisible by ell, so

`F_j′=−2P_j′`.

If this derivative were nonzero, its degree would be at most r/ell^j−2, while its selected-root multiplicities would sum to at least

`sum_c ceil(n_c/ell^j)−t ≥ M/ell^j−t`.

From (1), for every0≤j<a,

`M/ell^j > r/ell^j +(4s−2)ell^(a−1−j) ≥ r/ell^j+4s−2`.

Subtracting t≤4s makes the lower bound strictly greater than r/ell^j−2. Therefore F_j′=0. Every exponent of P_j is divisible by ell, and perfection of the residue field permits an ell-th root. Taking that root produces F_(j+1) of the required form; coefficients1 and−2 are fixed by Frobenius. This proves the induction through j=a.

At the final stage, `deg P_a<s`. Every occupied c remains a root of F_a, so P_a(c)=W_s(c). Since each occupied point carried at most q selected roots, there are at least ceil(M/q) occupied points, proving (2). The roots, the integral quotient coefficients, and all Frobenius roots lie in the residue field generated by μ_(4s). Alternatively, if the coordinates are in F_ell, at least s matches determine P_a by interpolation over F_ell.

A sharper, profile-dependent sufficient condition replaces (1) by

`sum_c ceil(n_c/ell^j) > r/ell^j+t−2` for every j<a.

The uniform condition (1) is convenient because it requires no information about the collapsed support profile. Equality in (1) is not included.

## Prime-power agreement bound

**Corollary.** If `r=ell^a` and ell>5, every characteristic-zero polynomial of degree below r agrees with W_r on at most

`r+2r/ell`

coordinates. Thus its capacity gap above rate1/4 is at most `1/(2ell)`.

Otherwise apply the theorem with s=1. The residual candidate is constant and agrees with W₁ at more than one of the four fourth roots. Those four values are0,2,i,−i and are pairwise distinct in characteristic ell>5, a contradiction. Since r/ell is an integer, the displayed bound has no rounding ambiguity. For a=1 it recovers M≤r+2.

Consequently a sequence of prime-power orders with prime bases tending to infinity cannot support a fixed positive characteristic-zero margin for these cyclic words. This does not exclude powers of a fixed small prime, nor arbitrary composite orders.

## What the general theorem does and does not isolate

For a fixed normalized capacity gap eta=(M−r)/(4r)>0, condition (1) is satisfied whenever

`ell > (1−1/(2s))/eta`.

A large prime-power divisor therefore forces a smaller cyclic source in that residue characteristic with at least the same normalized gap. It does **not** by itself exclude that larger order or prove that only smooth orders are possible. The smaller source may exist, and its alphabet need not be prime.

If s is prime, the residual nearest maximum exceeds s. For sufficiently large ell the four received bucket values remain distinct, so no nearest polynomial is constant; the μ_s orbit then has exactly s elements. If, in addition, s tends to infinity, ell≡1 modulo4s, and ell/s tends to infinity, the reduction supplies precisely the type of short prime-field nearest source sought for ordinary-CA amplification. These are additional arithmetic/asymptotic hypotheses, not conclusions of the reduction theorem.

For bounded s, the exact cyclotomic support-ideal gates can instead constrain the allowed residue characteristic. The existing r3/r5 all-split-prime certificates are examples. No unrestricted classification of those residue sources is asserted here.
