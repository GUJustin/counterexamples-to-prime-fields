% Asymmetric regular-collision core, with only the rich component amplified.
\subsection{An asymmetric collision bank}
\label{subsec:asymmetric-collision-bank}

A smaller component can supply the rich witnesses while a larger component
supplies additional core coordinates.  This gives a different polynomial-gap
tradeoff, with a prime alphabet only a polylogarithmic factor larger than
the domain.

\begin{theorem}[Asymmetric collision bank]
\label{thm:asymmetric-collision-bank}
There is a sequence of primes $p$ and length-$n$, dimension-three
Reed--Solomon codes over $\mathbb F_p$, with two received words $F,G$
and integers $A<T=A+d$, such that
\[
 \operatorname{agr}_3(F)=\operatorname{agr}_3(G)
 =\operatorname{CA}_3(F,G)=A,
 \qquad n a_1(3/n)<A<T<\sqrt{2n}.
\]
At least $2^{-19}p$ distinct parameters
$\lambda\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}$ have a singleton list at
agreement threshold $T$ for $(1-\lambda)F+\lambda G$.  The parameters
satisfy
\[
 d=\Theta\!\left(\frac{n^{1/3}}{(\log n)^2}\right),\qquad
 p=\Theta\bigl(n(\log n)^4\bigr),\qquad
 \frac{T-A}{n}=\Theta\!\left(\frac{n^{-2/3}}{(\log n)^2}\right).
\]
At threshold $A$, the list on every member of this affine line has
size at most $R+1$, where $R=\Theta(n^{1/3}(\log n)^2)$.
Successful witnesses may have more than $T$ agreements.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $K$ be an integer tending to infinity and put
\[
 H=\left\lfloor16K^{2/3}(\log K)^{4/3}\right\rfloor.
\]
For $x=H,K$, let $v_x$ count the primes in $[x/2,x]$, and define
\[
 t=\lfloor v_H/16\rfloor,\quad s=\lfloor v_K/16\rfloor,
 \quad R=t^2,\quad L=t^2+s^2,\quad
 M=\lfloor L/8\rfloor,\quad Q_0=RHM.
\]
These quantities are chosen before the field.  The prime number theorem
implies
\[
 v_x\sim\frac{x}{2\log x},\qquad
 \frac{Q_0}{K^4}\longrightarrow\frac9{8192}.
\]
Choose a prime $p\equiv1\pmod4$ in $(4096Q_0,8192Q_0)$, using the
prime number theorem in that fixed progression.  Then $p>K^4$
eventually.  Also $H<K/2$, so the two intervals of small primes are
disjoint.

A largest quartic class in each interval has enough primes to label
the two parts of $K_{t,t}$ and $K_{s,s}$, respectively.  All vertex
primes are distinct.  Within each component choose a reference prime
$z$ in its quartic class, and choose square factors satisfying
$\alpha_a^2=a/z$, $\beta_b^2=b/z$.  Each edge gives the square
parameter $\theta_{ab}=\alpha_a/\beta_b$, with $\theta_{ab}^2=a/b$.
Use the bank $P_\theta(X)=\theta+X^2/\theta$.

As in Theorem~\ref{thm:collision-bank-constant-fraction}, unique
factorization and $p>K^4$ show that a distinct-edge pair product has
at most two representations; the double representations are precisely
rectangle diagonals within one component.  Coherent factors make their
products exactly equal.  Give both square roots to a uniquely
represented pair, and one root to each pair at a double representation.
At each resulting core coordinate set the received value to its assigned
pair sum and set $g=0$.  Every core coordinate has exactly two bank
owners.  The $R=t^2$ words from the smaller component have exactly
\[
 A=2(L-1)-(t-1)^2=t^2+2s^2+2t-3
\]
core matches.  The other words have exactly
\[
 A'=2(L-1)-(s-1)^2=2t^2+s^2+2s-3.
\]
Thus
\[
 A-A'=(s-t)(s+t-2)\sim s^2,\qquad
 N_0=\frac{t^2 A+s^2 A'}2\sim\frac{s^4}2.
\]
Every quadratic outside the entire bank has at most $L$ core matches,
by counting its at most two roots against each bank member.

Use the translated grid $U=u_0+u$, $V=v_0+v$, $1\le u,v\le M$.
Keep nonzero square ratios $V/U$, choose one square root by a fixed
rule, remove repeated ratios, and remove core ratios.  For the retained
count $X$, the same elementary affine-equation estimates give
\[
 \mathbb E X\ge M^2\frac{(p-1)^2}{2p^2}
 -\frac{\binom{M^2}{2}}p-\frac{M^2\binom L2}p
 =\left(\frac12-o(1)\right)M^2.
\]
Here $L^2/p=O((\log K)^{-4})\to0$, so
$\Pr(X\ge M^2/4)\ge1/3-o(1)$.
On these coordinates put $g=1/U$ and $f=c_0/U$.

For a rich bank parameter, its labels are
\[
 \lambda+c_0=\theta u_0+v_0/\theta
                   +\frac{au+bv}{b\theta},
 \qquad a,b\in[H/2,H].
\]
They lie in an interval image of size at most $3HM<p$, and each fiber
has at most $1+2M/H\le3M/H$ points.  Set
$d=\lfloor M/(64H)\rfloor$.  If $X\ge M^2/4$, every rich word has
at least $HM/16$ labels with $d$ retained matches.  Cross-bank collisions
need be counted only among these $R$ rich banks, using all their raw
interval labels.  Their count $C$ obeys
\[
 \mathbb E C\le\frac{\binom R2(3HM)^2}{p},\qquad
 \Pr(C>RHM/64)<\frac{288}{4096}<\frac13.
\]
A translation therefore exists satisfying both requirements.  After
removing both bank-label pairs at each collision, at least $RHM/32$
rich labels remain, each with a unique rich owner.

The filler words cannot reach $A$ at any label.  Indeed, for them
$a,b\in[K/2,K]$, so their entire grid fiber at a fixed label has at most
$1+2M/K$ points.  The integer label interval has length at most
$3KM<p$, so this count has no modular wraparound.  Since
$A-A'\sim s^2$ while $1+2M/K=o(s^2)$, their core-plus-grid agreement
is strictly below $A$ uniformly in the label.

Choose $c_0\ne0$ outside all rich raw labels, whose union has size
at most $3RHM<p-2$.  The zero polynomial at $\lambda=-c_0$ is the
sole outsider exception and is discarded.  Any other nonbank quadratic
has at most $2M$ grid matches (a nonzero constant has at most one row),
in addition to its at most $L$ core matches.

Put $T=A+d$ and $n=\lfloor T^2/2\rfloor+1$.
Since $n\sim2s^4$ and $N_0+M^2\sim(1/2+1/64)s^4$, there is room
for neutral padding.  Choose the added coordinates outside the old
domain, zero, and all roots of $X^3-P_\theta$ for the whole bank;
there are at most $3L$ additional excluded points and $p\gg n$.
Give them $g=0$, $f=X^3$.  Banks gain nothing and outsiders gain at
most three.  Thus fillers remain below $A$, and all outsiders except
the discarded zero label have agreement at most $L+2M+3<A$.
Each remaining rich label has a singleton threshold list.

Outside the rich raw-label union and the zero exception, each word
has maximum agreement exactly $A$, attained by every rich bank word.
Choose two such finite labels as endpoints.  Ordinary CA is exactly
$A$: a zero explaining direction restricts to the core and neutral
block, whose maximum is $A$; a nonzero quadratic direction has at most
two matches there and at most $2M$ on the grid.  An invertible change
to the chosen endpoints preserves CA and bijectively parametrizes the
same affine line.  The number of singleton interior labels is at least
\[
 RHM/32-1>p/2^{18}-1\ge p/2^{19}
\]
eventually.  At the relaxed threshold only the $R$ rich bank words
and possibly the exceptional zero polynomial can occur.

Finally $n\sim2s^4=\Theta(K^4/(\log K)^4)$,
$d=\Theta(K^{4/3}/(\log K)^{10/3})$, and $p=\Theta(K^4)$.
These yield the stated formulas for $d,p,R$ and the normalized gap.
Both $A/\sqrt n$ and $T/\sqrt n$ tend to $\sqrt2$, whereas
\[
 n a_1(3/n)\le\sqrt{3n/2}+(3n/8)^{1/4}.
\]
Thus $A>n a_1(3/n)$ eventually, and $T^2<2n$ by definition.
\end{proof}

The rate remains $3/n$.  The normalized source gap tends to zero faster
than the first-order and capacity margins, which have order $n^{-1/2}$.
This is a different constant-fraction, growing-gap tradeoff, not a
fixed-rate or prescribed-domain assertion.
