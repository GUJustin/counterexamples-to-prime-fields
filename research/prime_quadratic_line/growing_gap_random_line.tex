\subsection{Growing source separation with quadratic witnesses}
\label{subsec:growing-gap-random-line}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:growing-gap-random-line}
For every sufficiently large prime $p$ there are a domain of size $n$ in
$\mathbb F_p$, two received words $F,G$, and integers $A,T$ such that
both words and their common agreement with degree-at-most-two polynomials
are exactly $A$, while at least $(1/e-o(1))p$ nonzero parameters $z$ have
a singleton list of polynomials agreeing with $F+zG$ on at least $T$ coordinates.
Moreover,
\[
 T^2<2n,\qquad T>n a_1(3/n),\qquad
 T-A\sim\frac{\log n}{4\log\log n},\qquad
 \frac pn\sim2e\log n\log\log n.
\]
Thus the number of these parameters is superlinear in $n$ and the absolute
source separation grows, with the polynomial degree fixed at two.
The rate and the relative source separation tend to zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first construct the quadratic intersection bank over a field of size
$O(L^2)$ or larger. Choose an odd prime $q\in[L,2L]$ and put
$b_i=2qi+(i^2\bmod q)$ for $0\le i<L$.
Equality of two pair sums of the $b_i$ forces equality of the sums of
the indices, since the difference of the two remainder sums has absolute
value less than $2q$. Reduction modulo $q$ then forces equality of the
unordered index pairs. Also $0\le b_i<8L^2$.
If $p-1>32L^2$ and $\gamma$ generates $\mathbb F_p^*$, set
$a_i=\gamma^{b_i}$ and $P_i(X)=X^2/a_i^2+a_i^2$.
The $L(L-1)$ points $\pm a_i a_j$ for $i<j$ are distinct, and the
$a_i^2$ are distinct. Assign the value $a_i^2+a_j^2$ at these points.
Every $P_i$ agrees on exactly $A=2L-2$ core points; a nonbank quadratic
agrees on at most $L$, by counting its roots against all $L$ bank polynomials.

Put $d=\lfloor\log p/(4\log\log p)\rfloor$ and
$L=\lfloor(p^d d!)^{1/(2d+1)}\rfloor$. Define
$T=2L-2+d$, $n=\lceil(T^2+1)/2\rceil$, and
$t=n-L(L-1)$. Stirling's formula gives
\[
 t=L^2+O(Ld+d^2),\quad
 \frac{L(t/p)^d}{d!}\longrightarrow1,\quad
 \frac pn\sim2e\log p\log\log p.
\]
In particular the core exists and we can choose $t$ additional distinct
nonzero coordinates outside it. Set $g=0$ on the core and $g(x)=x^3$
on the additional coordinates. Independently at each additional coordinate,
choose $f(x)$ uniformly outside
$\{P_i(x),P_i(x)-g(x):0\le i<L\}$; on the core use the assigned word.

Fix $\lambda\notin\{0,1\}$. Let $X_{i,\lambda}$ count additional
agreements of $P_i$ with $f+\lambda g$, and let
$Y_\lambda=\#\{i:X_{i,\lambda}\ge d\}$.
For each $i$, at most $6(L-1)$ coordinates forbid its desired value:
the equations causing a prohibition are
$P_i-P_j=\lambda X^3$ or $P_i-P_j=(\lambda-1)X^3$.
At all remaining coordinates its probability is between $1/p$ and
$1/(p-2L)$. Different incumbents have distinct desired values.
For every fixed positive integer $k$, selecting disjoint $d$-sets of
agreements for $k$ specified incumbents gives the bounds
\[
 \frac{(t-6L-kd)_{kd}}{(d!)^k p^{kd}}
 \left(1-\frac{k}{p-2L}\right)^{t-kd}
 \ \le\ \Pr(X_{i_1,\lambda},\ldots,X_{i_k,\lambda}\ge d)
 \ \le\ \frac{(t)_{kd}}{(d!)^k(p-2L)^{kd}}.
\]
Here $(u)_v$ denotes a falling factorial. For the lower bound require
exactly the selected matches and no others for these incumbents.
Multiplication by $(L)_k$ shows
$\mathbb E(Y_\lambda)_k=1+o(1)$ uniformly in $\lambda$.
For every fixed positive integer $m$, the pointwise Bonferroni inequality
\[
 \mathbf1_{Y=1}\ge
 \sum_{j=1}^{2m}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}(Y)_j}{(j-1)!}
\]
therefore gives a uniform lower limit of
$\sum_{r=0}^{2m-1}(-1)^r/r!$ for $\Pr(Y_\lambda=1)$.
Letting $m$ tend to infinity after $p$ proves
$\Pr(Y_\lambda=1)\ge1/e-o(1)$.

A union bound over all at most $p^4$ quadratic/parameter pairs shows that
the probability any nonbank quadratic has $L-1$ additional matches is at most
\[
 p^4\binom{t}{L-1}(p-2L)^{-(L-1)}=o(1).
\]
Consequently some realization has no such pair and has at least
$(1/e-o(1))p$ singleton threshold lists. Every nonbank polynomial then
has total agreement at most $A$, uniformly in the parameter. The blacklist
ensures that $f$ and $f+g$ each have agreement exactly $A$.
Any nonzero quadratic agrees with $g$ at at most two core points and three
additional points. The zero polynomial confines common agreements to the
core, whose maximum is $A$. Thus the common agreement is exactly $A$.

Finally set $F=f$ and $G=f+g$.
For $z\ne-1$, the substitution $\lambda=z/(1+z)$ and multiplication
by $1+z$ preserve the lists, and the counted parameters become nonzero.
At $z=-1$, the word $-g$ additionally has singleton threshold list $\{0\}$.
The asserted asymptotics follow from the parameter choice.
Finally $T/\sqrt n\to\sqrt2$, whereas
$n a_1(3/n)/\sqrt n\to\sqrt{3/2}$, proving the first-order inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The same argument permits any fixed $0<c\le1/2$ with
$d=\lfloor c\log p/\log\log p\rfloor$. In this case
\[
 T-A\sim\frac{c\log n}{\log\log n},\qquad
 \frac pn\sim\frac{e}{2c}(\log n)^{1/(2c)-1}\log\log n,
\]
and the singleton fraction remains at least $1/e-o(1)$.
In particular $c=1/2$ gives separation asymptotic to
$\log n/(2\log\log n)$ and $\Omega(n\log\log n)$ singleton parameters.
The factorial-moment bounds above require $t/p\to0$, not $d t/p\to0$,
so this endpoint is included.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{An exact finite existence certificate.}
For $p=18120497$, the parameters $n=4503001$, $K=3$, $A=2998$,
and $T=3001$ admit at least $4680342$ singleton exceptional challenges,
more than $n$. Both source agreements and common agreement are exactly
$A$. Exact rational factorial-moment bounds and a nonbank union bound
certify existence; this certificate does not specify a sampled received
word. The independent replay is in
