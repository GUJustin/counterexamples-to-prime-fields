# Next structural target after deterministic covers

The conic-bank route now has an audited upper bound matched in order by
explicit examples. Repeating its degree/length scale alone cannot close the
remaining gap to the general DKT exceptional-count bound. Covers increase
absolute source separation but preserve vanishing normalized separation and
rate; do not present further cover parameter tuning as a conceptual escape.

A genuinely different lower-bound route needs a bank with more simultaneous
agreements per core coordinate than the constant-richness pair/triple banks,
or a mechanism not organized around such a bank. The previously tested
elliptic-normal-quartic cone specialization did not supply this. The known
characteristic-zero incidence restrictions do not automatically rule out
prime-field constructions that depend essentially on modular geometry.

For the quadratic coefficient model, a received coordinate (x,f(x)) defines
the plane a*x²+b*x+c=f(x). A new core would need a large coefficient point
set with many incidences with these planes, whose normals are constrained
to (x²,x,1), and sufficiently uniform degrees to force high agreement for
many candidate polynomials. Merely finding rich arbitrary planes is not
enough. The dimension-three full coefficient geometry and its normal-curve
constraint are the useful next target, with exact nonbank separation still
required. A candidate must be compared to the existing list literature
before novelty is claimed.

On the upper-bound side, persistent affine witness graphs give a valid
construction-specific counting refinement, but small attained lists on one
line do not control the number of those graphs. DKT's applicable geometric
budget is not a supplied singleton-list parameter. Its codewide list lemma
cannot be invoked with the line-wide bound one: a different received word
on the same domain has at least L witnesses at the target threshold.
