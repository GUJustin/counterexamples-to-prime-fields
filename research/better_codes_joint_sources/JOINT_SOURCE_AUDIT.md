## 2. Linear independence is insufficient, even for actual sources

Every truncated ConstraintKernel is closed under multiplication by the challenge variable Z, provided the total cap is increased accordingly: local equations remain zero after multiplication by Z, the contact budget is unchanged, and the derivative cap is unchanged. Thus Q and ZQ are linearly independent over the base field when Q is nonzero, yet have the same relevant zero locus away from Z=0 and the same common polynomial factor Q.

An explicit actual-source example is available over F5 on domain {0,1}. Let

`E(X)=2X³−3X²+X`,
`Q0=Y−X−Z+E(X)(1−R)`.

Then E(x)=0 and E'(x)=1 on the domain. For received line w_x(Z)=x+Z, its zeroth local block is zero and its first block is a−R. Hence Q0 and ZQ0 both belong to the multiplicity-two kernel with contact cap D_contact=4, code weight w=1, derivative cap S=1 and total cap L=2. They are independent, but their common zero locus still contains the whole hypersurface Q0=0. The degree-one pencil P_z=X+z is an actual solution of both equations.

More generally, modulo an irreducible F, the vectors Z,Z²,...,Z^k can have arbitrarily large linear rank while all vanish on the same divisor Z=0. Even independence of the source restrictions modulo F does not imply a proper second intersection.

## 3. The exact extra condition that would suffice

Work over an infinite field, or a sufficiently large finite field, in the coordinate domain

`A = k[X,Y,R,Z]/(F)`

localized away from any fixed components that are being counted separately. Let V be the image of the source kernel and let J be the ideal generated by V. If J has height at least two, there exist q1,q2 in V such that their common zero locus has codimension at least two in Spec A.

Proof: choose q1 nonzero. Its principal divisor has finitely many height-one minimal primes. Since J has height at least two, V is not contained in any of these primes. Avoid the corresponding finite union of proper linear subspaces to choose q2. Then no height-one prime contains both q1 and q2; the principal ideal theorem gives the desired proper intersection. Over a finite field it suffices that its cardinality exceed the number of primes being avoided. Nilpotent/multiplicity issues can be handled by working in the integral quotient by irreducible F and using minimal primes of the principal ideal.

This is a conditional algebraic lemma. A usable certificate needs BOTH:

1. a proof that no unaccounted height-one prime of A contains the entire source image; and
2. a new joint-intersection/seed-count bound, with its actual degree cost shown to fit the final budget.

Neither follows from `dim K≥gap`, from two independent vectors, or from the existing condition that F does not divide every source vector. The latter only says J is nonzero, i.e. excludes height zero in A.

## 4. Why the height condition needs careful localization

All source polynomials vanish on the received ordinary fibers `(X=x,Y=w_x(Z))`. If F contains one such fiber, it is an unavoidable height-one component inside Spec A. A global proper-intersection assertion is false until these fixed fibers are removed, for example by localizing away from the domain locator Λ(X). Candidate polynomial graphs dominate the X-line, so this localization does not discard an entire candidate graph.

There can also be horizontal common components. In the explicit example, **every** source polynomial satisfying the usual contact budget vanishes on the affine-pencil surface `Y=X+Z,R=1`, because every P_z=X+z agrees everywhere. This surface persists after localizing away from Λ and is a height-one component inside F=Q0. Thus even the entire kernel can have a common divisor on F for a genuine geometric reason; its existence does not signal a missed dimension count. Full-support badness permits such pencil families to be counted separately, but they cannot simply be declared absent from the algebraic base locus.

A promising precise new theorem would classify every surviving common source component as an affine-pencil surface (or another explicitly bounded seed family). It would then remove/count those components and apply the conditional two-source lemma on the complement. That is a substantial base-locus theorem, not an arithmetic tweak to the current receipt.
