# Support-incidence bound: an attributed improvement to puncturing

## Status and attribution

**Proved elementary consequence of prior work; no novelty claim.** Sunghyeon Jo, *Reed–Solomon Mutual Correlated Agreement Beyond the Johnson Radius*, [IACR ePrint 2026/1432](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1432), Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 4.2, supplies the sharp local rejection bound and the affine bad-parameter envelope. The primary PDF was downloaded and its full proofs checked. It was received July 13, 2026; the August 19 update says its mathematics is unchanged. Chojecki's [2026/1463](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1463) explicitly attributes its all-test-size envelope to Jo.

The weighted witness-pair and degree-e versions below are direct extensions of Jo's incidence argument. They should be presented as such, rather than as new independent theorems. The stronger bound gives a useful correction to the manuscript's comparison of available universal bounds.

## Statement

Let C be a linear [n,k] MDS code over a finite field F of size q, with 1≤k<n. Let f(z)=sum_(j=0)^e z^j f_j, and fix k+1≤t≤n. A pair (z,c), where z∈F and c∈C, is counted when its entire agreement set

S(z,c)={i:c_i=f(z)_i}

has size at least t and the coefficient words f_0,...,f_e cannot all be explained by codewords on S(z,c). Equivalently, at least one f_j restricted to S(z,c) lies outside C restricted to S(z,c).

For every k+1≤b≤t, let T_b be the number of b-coordinate sets on which at least one coefficient word is outside the punctured code. Then

    sum_(counted pairs (z,c)) binom(|S(z,c)|−1,b−1)
       ≤ min(e,q) T_b
       ≤ min(e,q) binom(n,b).

Consequently the number of counted witness pairs is at most

    floor[min(e,q) min_(k+1≤b≤t) binom(n,b)/binom(t−1,b−1)].

For affine lines, e=1. If the coefficient words have no common codeword explanation on any t-set, every nearby witness is counted. There is no list-size multiplier. The statement applies to all witnesses at each parameter, not only a selected witness or distinct parameters.

## Proof

**Local rejection abundance (Jo Lemma 4.1).** If w lies outside an [m,k] MDS code and k+1≤b≤m, at least binom(m−1,b−1) b-subsets reject w. Here is a short alternative deletion proof. Induct on m for fixed b, starting with m=b, where the one full set rejects. If deleting some coordinate x makes w a codeword restriction, take its unique global extension c. It disagrees with w at x, so every b-set containing x rejects: otherwise the explaining codeword agrees with c at b−1≥k positions and hence equals c, a contradiction. If all m single-coordinate deletions reject, induction and counting occurrences of a rejecting b-set in the m−b deletions that contain it give

    (m−b) R_b ≥ m binom(m−2,b−1)
                  > (m−b) binom(m−1,b−1).

The weak claimed bound follows. A one-coordinate perturbation of a codeword attains equality, so this local ingredient cannot be improved uniformly.

**Witness incidence.** For each counted pair choose any coefficient word that is outside the code on S. The local lemma provides at least binom(|S|−1,b−1) rejecting b-subsets of S. For a fixed b-set A that rejects at least one coefficient, the vector polynomial

    f(z)|A modulo C|A

is formally nonzero and has degree at most e. Apply a linear functional nonzero on one of its nonzero coefficient vectors. Every parameter at which f(z)|A is in C|A is then a root of a fixed nonzero scalar polynomial of degree at most e. There are at most min(e,q) such parameters. For each parameter, the b-set determines at most one global codeword because b≥k and C is MDS. This bounds all incidences and proves the result. The argument remains valid when e≥q. For e=0 the count is zero.

## Comparison with the current puncturing bound

Use manuscript notation a=n−k, a/2≤r<a, s=a−r, d=2r−a, and t=n−r=k+s. Define

    B_puncture=(n−d) binom(n,d)/binom(r,d),
    B_circuit=binom(n,k+1)/binom(k+s−1,k).

In this radius range, the optimal test size is always b=k+1. Indeed for R_b=binom(n,b)/binom(t−1,b−1),

    R_(b+1)/R_b = b(n−b)/[(b+1)(t−b)],

which is at least one exactly when b(n−t+1)≥t. Here (k+1)(r+1)−t=(k+2)r+1−a>0. Thus R_b is increasing for b≥k+1. The improved manuscript bound is the minimum of the two *rational* bounds, followed by the floor; for degree e multiply the minimum by e before flooring.

Examples are generated by verify_support_incidence.py. At n=10,k=2,r=6, puncturing gives 84 while incidence gives 40. At r=a−1 the two bounds coincide with binom(n,k+1), so the endpoint extremality remains unchanged. At the unique-radius boundary, puncturing can be much stronger; neither should simply replace the other everywhere.

For general t, the minimizing test size can be found without a scan:

    b*=min(t,max(k+1,ceil(t/(n−t+1)))).

If t/(n−t+1) is an integer, the adjacent next test size ties whenever it is in range.

## Limitations and an obstruction to a routine puncturing improvement

At fixed positive rate k/n→rho and fixed agreement fraction t/n→tau with 0<rho<tau<1, the smallest-test bound has natural-log exponent

    H(rho)−tau H(rho/tau)>0,

where H is binary entropy with natural logarithms. In the manuscript's beyond-unique-radius range it is the optimal incidence test. Hence this improvement remains exponential and does not establish an O(n) or polynomial numerator at every fixed gap. The sharp local rejection example means that further improvement must use compatibility among the different witnesses, not merely a larger local rejection lower bound.

Puncturing first and then applying this smallest-test incidence bound cannot improve it. Deleting u≤r coordinates and padding each error support to an r-set gives the multiplicative factor

    binom(n,u)/binom(r,u) * binom(n−u,k+1)/binom(t−1,k)
      = B_circuit * binom(n−k−1,u)/binom(r,u)
      ≥ B_circuit,

provided the protected unexplained (k+1)-set is retained, as in the manuscript. This rules out one natural combination of the two arguments.
