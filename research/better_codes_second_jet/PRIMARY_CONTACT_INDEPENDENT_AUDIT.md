# Independent audit of the primary-factor contact resource

Verdict: **PASS** for the subset-factor bound, uniform lower bound, quotient budget, and the stronger average/additive version in PRIMARY_FACTOR_CONTACT_BUDGET.md. These are genuine additional constraints on primary factors; none is yet a bound on the geometric normal divisor.

## Subset-factor proof

Work over k(Z) and, if necessary, adjoin the pencil parameter c as an indeterminate. For each w-element node subset T, the interpolation polynomial P_T has X degree<w and the locator H_T has degree w. Therefore P_T+cH_T has degree<=w and derivative degree<=w−1. Every claimed local contact order is inherited under this polynomial substitution, and the resulting X degree is at most the source's weighted degree W.

If the contact sum exceeds W, the substituted polynomial is identically zero. Over k(X,Z) its affine jet line consequently divides J. Distinct T give different lines because H_T'/H_T has simple poles, with nonzero residue one, at exactly the nodes of T. This remains valid in every characteristic: squarefree locators cannot lose these poles. The factors are pairwise nonassociate, so their number is bounded even by deg_R J, and hence by floor(W/(w−1)). No finite-field density or generic-rank assumption is needed.

The uniform corollary follows by fixing w−1 nodes, and the factor quotient has contact at least (m−a_x)_+. Local substitution is injective after inverting t (solve for its independent E variable), so t-adic orders add on products. Weighted degrees add as well. These facts justify the quotient and product formulas, including the use of the maximum actual contact rather than its minimum.

## Average-contact strengthening

Here is an equivalent exact derivation useful for auditing the new averaging step. Suppose u_x<=U and d=floor(W/(w−1)). Let C=binom(n,w). At most d subsets have contact sum>W. The average subset sum is (w/n)sum_x u_x, and its excess above W is at most d*w*U/C. If positive, that excess is a rational number with denominator dividing n and is at least1/n. Hence

    C>n*d*w*U  implies  W>=(w/n)sum_x u_x.

For an actual nonzero first-jet polynomial, u_x<=W: after substitution, every t exponent is at most the original monomial weight, since w>=2 and a+2i<=a+w i+(w−1)j. Injectivity prevents an identically zero substituted polynomial. Thus U=W is legitimate for every factor.

At the pinned benchmark all factor weights are below2^25. With n2^18,w<2^17 and w−1>2^16, the right side n*d*w*U is below2^69. The elementary lower bound binom(n,w)>=binom(n,5)>2^75 suffices. Therefore every irreducible factor F_i has nonnegative charge

    e_i=wt(F_i)−(w/n)sum_x ord_x(F_i)>=0.

Counting multiplicities in the primary factorization and using its total contact>=m at every node gives

    sum_i e_i <=wt(Q0)−mw <m(A−w).

This is stronger than a maximum-contact bound and is additive. Factors depending only on the challenge have weight and generic local contact zero, hence charge zero; they do not invalidate the argument.

The independent capped-quotient formulation is also valid: if WJ=wt(Q0/F), B=floor(WJ/(w−1)), and binom(n−1,w−1)>B*m*w², then

    sum_x min(ord_x F,m)>=nm−floor(n*WJ/w).

It follows by averaging the quotient's contacts (m−a_x)_+ and using their bound m. This form retains the primary multiplicity cutoff explicitly.

For the binding-shaped locator model E55, its exact factor weight is55n and every contact is55, so its charge is55(n−w)=7209015. The primary budget at m118 is118(A−w)=5924072. The factor is therefore excluded even though its raw weight14417920 is below mA21390450. The quoted deficit1284943 is correct.

## Remaining quantitative bridge

The additive charge belongs to an entire irreducible primary factor. A simple first-tail component is a codimension-two component inside that factor's surface, not itself a factor to which another copy of e_i can be assigned. Consequently summing e_i over such components would double-count an unproved resource. A valid retained refinement needs a bound on the aggregate normal cost of the active simple components of one F in terms of its one charge e(F), its existing degree caps, and the second-source support/contact data.

Nor does ideal membership alone supply that bound. The retained equations live in a localization of the prolongation ideal generated by F and delta F. Clearing its separant denominators can change weighted degree and local contact. A lower bound on the weights of the displayed generators does not bound the weight of every polynomial in the localized ideal: cancellation and saturation must be controlled. No such estimate is proved by the subset argument.

Thus the new contact resource rigorously removes the expensive binding-shaped locator countermodel, but it has not yet reduced the retained normal term at the binding context. It is a meaningful input for a new charging lemma, not a completed geometric or benchmark certificate.
