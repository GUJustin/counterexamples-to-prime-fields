## Why common-factor avoidance needs a new alternative

The upper-bound-route agent identified the sharper useful gate. For a first-order irreducible factor F with F_R nonzero modulo F, put

    delta F=F_X+R F_Y+2V F_R.

A pair of curvature sources supplies a helper for F only if its V-resultant is nonzero modulo F. Global rational independence is insufficient. If the entire source kernel lies in the prolongation ideal

    (F,delta F),

then modulo F all source pairs lie in the one-dimensional module generated by delta F, so every pairwise resultant is divisible by F. Yet F need not divide the individual sources. This is distinct from the incumbent first-order universal-factor branch.

More precisely, after localizing at 2F_R, the condition for Q=A V+B is the linear congruence

    A(F_X+R F_Y)−2F_R B = 0 mod F.

This gives an independently checkable proposed replacement for the old escape lemma: either some source violates this congruence and supplies a first-order helper via elimination, or the whole kernel satisfies it and one must bound that prolongation-module branch. The latter cannot be replaced by ordinary divisibility by F.

A possible dimension approach must count source vectors in intersections of these prolongation modules, including their contact constraints. Ambient dimension alone already fails by millions of times in JOINT_CURVATURE_MODULE_GATE.md. Locally the module is generated by F and delta F with their Koszul relation; for homogeneous generators forming a regular sequence, the corresponding singly graded quotient has the complete-intersection Hilbert series. Applying this to nonhomogeneous generators requires a justified homogenization or associated-graded comparison. The actual benchmark has slope, jet, challenge and weighted caps simultaneously, so a scalar weighted Hilbert series is not an established certificate for the required truncated module. Nor does it alone control arbitrary intersections over several F.

This identifies the new mathematical obligation precisely: a contact-compatible Hilbert/rank bound for the prolongation-module alternative, or a separate counting theorem for the regular second-order branch. The known ordinary-core examples rule out deriving such a theorem just from nonzero coefficient A or primitive normalization. The conditional resultant helper cost is small according to the upper agent; the algebraic escape gate, rather than naive degree doubling, is the unresolved issue.
