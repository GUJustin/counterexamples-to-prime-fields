# Independent audit of the arbitrary-word elliptic translation barrier

Status: PASS for the stated symmetrized-translation polynomial family and its polynomial pullbacks/common factors. Audited source: `ARBITRARY_WORD_POLE_BARRIER.md`.

The Laurent coefficients 2t, 6t^2+2A, and 10t^3+6At+4B are correct. If all pair differences after denominator clearing had degree at most 2L-3, arbitrary label-dependent scalars and constants would make all three Laurent coefficients common. The second cannot vanish for L>=4; their ratio would place L distinct parameters on one nonzero cubic. Thus the minimal degree after any common polynomial subtraction is at least 2L-2. Choosing one candidate as the subtracted polynomial shows that maximum pair degree indeed equals this minimal maximum degree.

At each cleared pole, exactly the other L-1 candidates coincide; the exceptional candidate differs because its original pole is genuine. Off the poles, a normalized pair difference has nonzero numerator of degree at most four. The incidence Cauchy bound S^2<=M(S+4L(L-1)) and its stated relaxation follow. With D>=2L-2 and D<N, the displayed capacity-gap estimate is valid for an arbitrary received word and arbitrary affine domain. No density or torsion assumption is used.

For polynomial composition of degree e and common multiplication of degree z, pair degrees become at least e(2L-2)+z, off-pole pair intersections at most 4e, and exceptional node contributions at most e(L-1)+z per candidate on average. Common-factor zeros at pole preimages can be charged to z as well, so overlap does not invalidate the inequality. Interleaved polynomial operations collapse to a single composition and common factor. Their stated vanishing-margin conclusion follows uniformly in e and z.

Scope qualification: the final Lattes/rational-cover sentence is conceptual, rather than a proved extension of the polynomial-composition degree estimate. Arbitrary rational composition with denominator clearing and projective normalization needs its own divisor accounting. The audited theorem excludes neither general elliptic constructions nor higher-complexity functions sharing a polar divisor.
