\subsection{Finite extension alphabets on selected base-field domains}
\label{subsec:extension-native-short-domains}

The same compiler has finite instances at length $2^{18}$ and dimension
$2^{17}$ over cubic, quartic, quintic, and sextic alphabets.  Their
evaluation points remain in the prime subfield.  The assertion is the
existence of a selected union of fibers; it does not specify a list of
tags or establish the profile on a prescribed NTT subgroup.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:extension-native-selected-fibers}
Let $p$ be prime, $d\ge2$, $E=\mathbb F_{p^d}$, and $m\ge2$ divide
$p-1$.  Choose $b\in E$ of degree $d$ over $\mathbb F_p$, and put
\[
 P=(p-1)/m-1,\qquad M=p^d-1,\qquad
 \varepsilon=\frac{d\sqrt p+1}{P}.
\]
Let $2\le r\le s\le P$ and $0\le w<m$, and set
\[
 n=(s+1)m,\qquad J=(r-2)m+w+1.
\]
Suppose an integer $t\ge0$, with $s_0=s-2t$ and $r_0=r-t$,
satisfies the finite criterion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-weight-product-completion} for the displayed
$P,M,\varepsilon$, in particular $Mh_t<1$.
Then there are an $n$-point set $D\subset\mathbb F_p^*$ and words
$f,g:D\to E$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{agr}_J(f)=\operatorname{agr}_J(g)
 =\operatorname{CA}_J(f,g)=J+m-1,
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{agr}_J(f+\lambda g)=J+2m-1
 \qquad(\lambda\in E^*).
\]
The domain is a union of $s+1$ fibers of $X\mapsto X^m$.
Thus all $|E|-2$ non-endpoint affine mixtures of the two far sources
are at exactly the tested distance.  The capacity margin and source
separation are, respectively, $(2m-1)/n$ and $m/n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $H=(\mathbb F_p^*)^m$ and reserve $a_0\in H$.
For every nontrivial character $\chi$ of $E^*$ and every character
$\psi$ of $\mathbb F_p^*$, Katz's character-sum estimate on the
finite etale algebra $E\times\mathbb F_p$, at its regular element
$(b,0)$, gives~\cite[Theorem~2 and its proof]{katz1989-character-sums}
\[
 \left|\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^*}\chi(b-a)\psi(a)\right|
 \le d\sqrt p
 .
\]
The algebra has dimension $d+1$; the full-degree hypothesis makes the
$d$ conjugates of $b$ distinct and nonzero, and the nontriviality of
$\chi$ ensures geometric nontriviality.  Averaging over the $m$
characters trivial on $H$, and then removing $a_0$, bounds every
nontrivial character mean of
\[
 \mathcal A=\{b-a:a\in H\setminus\{a_0\}\}\subset E^*
\]
by $\varepsilon$.  This population has exactly $P$ distinct elements.
The finite completion lemma supplies $s$ distinct tags
$G\subset H\setminus\{a_0\}$ whose exact $r$-element products
of factors $b-a$ cover $E^*$.

Set $Y=X^m$, $D=Y^{-1}(G\cup\{a_0\})$, and let $R$ be the monic
locator of $w$ points in the reserved fiber $Y^{-1}(a_0)$.
Define
\[
 f=R\frac{Y^r-b^r}{Y-b},\qquad g=-\frac R{Y-b}.
\]
The denominator has no base-field root.  The monic polynomial $f$
has degree $J+m-1$.  For $\deg h<J$, the nonzero cleared numerator
$-R-(Y-b)h$ has degree at most $J+m-1$ as well.  Interpolating the
two quotient values on any $r-1$ tags, and multiplying by $R$,
gives simultaneous degree-at-most-$(J-1)$ witnesses with exactly
$w+(r-1)m=J+m-1$ common agreements.  This proves the source and
common-agreement equalities.

For $\lambda\ne0$, choose $S\subset G$, $|S|=r$, with
$V_S(b)=-\lambda$, and put $P_S(Z)=Z^r-V_S(Z)$.
The witness
\[
 h_S=R\frac{P_S(Y)-P_S(b)}{Y-b}
\]
has degree at most $w+(r-2)m=J-1$ and residual
$RV_S(Y)/(Y-b)$, which vanishes exactly on the core and the $r$
selected fibers.  This gives $w+rm=J+2m-1$ agreements.
For any other degree-below-$J$ polynomial $h$,
\[
 (Y-b)(f+\lambda g-h)
 =R(Y^r-b^r-\lambda)-(Y-b)h
\]
is monic of degree $J+2m-1$, since the second term has degree at
most $J+m-1$.  Root counting proves the matching upper bound.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Four exact finite instances.}
Put
\[
 p_G=2^{64}-2^{32}+1,\qquad
 p_B=2013265921,\qquad p_K=2130706433.
\]
All four cases below have $n=262144$, $J=131072$, and $w=m-1$.
Write $A=J+m-1$ and $T=J+2m-1$ for the exact source/common and
nonzero-pencil agreements.  The parameter $t$ counts completion pairs.
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r|r|r|c|c}
 E&m&t&A&T&\text{capacity margin}&\text{source separation}\\\hline
 \mathbb F_{p_G^3}&1024&4&132095&133119&2047/262144&1/256\\
 \mathbb F_{p_B^4}&1024&6&132095&133119&2047/262144&1/256\\
 \mathbb F_{p_B^5}&1024&22&132095&133119&2047/262144&1/256\\
 \mathbb F_{p_K^6}&512&0&131583&132095&1023/262144&1/512
\end{array}
\]
For the first three cases, $(s,r)=(255,129)$; for the last,
$(s,r)=(511,257)$.  The proof uses the upper bounds
$\sqrt{p_G}<2^{32}$, $\sqrt{p_B}<44870$, and
$\sqrt{p_K}<46160$.  Exact rational evaluation of the seed majorant
$(1+\varepsilon)^{s_0}$ and the completion recurrence gives,
in table order,
\[
 Mh_t<2^{-21},\qquad Mh_t<2^{-2},\qquad
 Mh_t<2^{-2},\qquad Mh_t<2^{-86}.
\]
Every recurrence step is rounded upward to denominator $2^{512}$.
These inequalities are integer comparisons, not floating-point tests.
Primality is certified by Proth witnesses $7,11,3$, respectively:
$p_G-1=(2^{32}-1)2^{32}$, $p_B-1=15\cdot2^{27}$, and
$p_K-1=127\cdot2^{24}$.

The script and exact receipts are supplied in
\nolinkurl{research/norm_quotient_coverage/verify_extension_native_short_domains.py}
and \nolinkurl{extension_native_short_domains_verified.json}.
They certify existence of the selected tag sets through the finite
lemma, without enumerating a domain or an extension field.  The
characteristic exceeds $J$ in every case.  These statements do not
provide the prescribed-domain agreement threshold $139782$.  Their
contribution is a finite existence guarantee at the displayed fields and
length, not a larger asymptotic distance from capacity than prior
inverse-logarithmic counterexamples.  They establish neither a
better.codes improvement nor tightness of the prime-field upper bounds.


\paragraph{Counting ceiling for this construction.}
This compiler assigns one challenge label to each $r$-element subset
of its $s$ tags.  Covering $E^*$ therefore requires
\[
 |E|-1\le\binom{s}{r}\le2^s.
\]
Consequently its capacity margin satisfies
\[
 \frac{2m-1}{n}<\frac{2}{s+1}
 \le\frac{2}{1+\log_2(|E|-1)}.
\]
At the fixed length $n=2^{18}$, the condition $n=(s+1)m$ makes $m$
a power of two.  If $m\ge2048$, then $s\le127$, whereas the exact
integer comparison
\[
 \binom{127}{63}<2013265921^4-1
\]
holds.  The right side is the smallest native multiplicative-group
size in the table.  Thus none of the four fields can have full native
coverage with $m\ge2048$ through these subset witnesses, regardless
of how the tags are chosen or coverage is proved.  The first three
rows already attain the largest permitted $m=1024$ and capacity
margin $2047/262144$ in this model.  The last row has counting room
for at most that same margin.  This is a limitation of the displayed
witness family, not an upper bound on all nearby codewords or on
other counterexample constructions.
