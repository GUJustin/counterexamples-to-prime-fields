# Binary-inspired transfer checkpoint, September 18 evening

The requested prime-alphabet superlinear exceptional count with substantial relative loss remains unproved. No better.codes improvement follows from this session. The official benchmark was rechecked at https://better.codes/ on September 19 UTC (September 18 Eastern): 68.11 to 116.13 bits, unchanged.

## What was actually checked

- The two-head binary hash can be compressed to a prime-field label with controlled collisions. This is already committed; it does not transfer the split supports.
- Increasing code dimension for the literal odd-characteristic signed product gives an exact identity, but the proposed domain cannot hold two different sparse locators. The strict dimension convention was corrected before publication.
- Adding a third high coefficient removes that immediate intersection contradiction. However, a fixed-domain factor recurrence then forces complementary supports to be a fixed set plus one complete multiplicative fiber. The proposed family has at most five members. Root independently checked the coefficient recurrence and the compiler identity.
- A different long-tail cancellation gives a genuine two-parameter identity. Root checked it symbolically, and an independent agent checked a cubic-incidence bound: for threshold T=4K, common-agreement upper bound A=3K+D, and D<K, its bank has at most 9n(n−A)/(T(T−A)) parameter points. This is bounded independently of K at fixed n/K and D/K<1. No finite seed scan was started after that check.
- The full-coefficient multiplicative-net candidate was checked against its primary subgroup-intersection theorem; the fixed finite embedding verifier passed. It does not produce the needed growing richness.

These are decisions about specific proposed constructions, not new lower bounds. They have not been appended to the main paper as results.

## Change in search direction

Stop treating low-degree formulas in two scalar parameters as replacements for the binary support family. Their algebraic incidence bounds prevent the required population in the tested constant-fraction regime. The binary construction has substantially more structure: the split-locator coefficients depend on the support through algebraic relations whose complexity grows with the domain.

The remaining constructive work must retain such growing complexity, or use a genuinely different bank with more freedom. An independent task is testing growing-message-dimension versions of the integer-grid constructions with the full first-order threshold and the relative loss explicitly accounted for. Merely raising the code dimension in the existing quadratic construction is not a valid improvement.

The previously proved extension-field puncturing result and prime-field linear-count bank remain the positive baseline. Neither is being relabeled as the missing prime-alphabet breakthrough.
