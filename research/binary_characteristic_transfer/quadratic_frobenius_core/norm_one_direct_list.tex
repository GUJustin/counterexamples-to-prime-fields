\subsection{A norm-one list with a matched finite upper bound}
\label{sec:norm-one-direct-list}

The next example concerns the size of one list, rather than the number
of exceptional parameters on a received line. Its incidence parameters
are those of the classical projective-plane design, with each coordinate
doubled. We make no novelty claim for that geometry.
The uniform comparison below uses an elementary shortening argument,
rather than a high-order interpolation certificate.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:quadratic-shortening-list}
On any $N$ distinct evaluation coordinates over any field, the number
of polynomials of degree at most two agreeing with a word on at least
$T$ coordinates is at most
\[
 U(N,T):=\frac{N(N-1)(T-2)}
 {T\bigl((T-1)^2-(N-1)\bigr)},
 \qquad T\ge3,\quad (T-1)^2>N-1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose exactly $T$ agreement coordinates for each of $M$ witnesses,
and let $r_x$ count the chosen sets containing $x$.
For $r_x>0$, after removing $x$, any two of these sets intersect in at most
one coordinate. Counting their incidences on the remaining $N-1$
coordinates and applying Cauchy's inequality gives
\[
 \frac{r_x(T-1)^2}{N-1}-(T-1)\le r_x-1,
 \qquad
 r_x\le\frac{(N-1)(T-2)}{(T-1)^2-(N-1)}.
\]
Summing $MT=\sum_xr_x$ proves the claim. This is the usual Johnson
count applied after fixing one agreement coordinate. The expression
$U(N,T)$ decreases with real $T\ge3$ wherever its denominator is positive:
the derivative has the sign of
$-\bigl(T(T-2)^2+N-2\bigr)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:norm-one-direct-list}
Let $p\ge53$ be prime, let $E=\mathbb F_{p^3}$, and put
\[
 L=p^2+p+1,\qquad N=2L,\qquad
 G=\ker\operatorname{Norm}_{E/\mathbb F_p},\qquad
 D=\{x\in E^*:x^2\in G\}.
\]
The dimension-three Reed--Solomon code on $D$ has a received word
$f(x)=x^{2p+2}$ whose maximum agreement is $A=2p+2$ and whose complete
list at that threshold consists of the $L=N/2$ polynomials
\[
 Q_a(X)=aX^2-a^{p^2+1},\qquad
 \operatorname{Norm}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(a)=-1.
\]
There are exactly $2L$ further quadratics
\[
 (uX-u^{p^2+1})^2,\qquad \operatorname{Norm}(u)\in\{1,-1\},
\]
each with exactly $p+1$ matches. Every quadratic outside these two
banks has at most four matches. Thus the complete list has size $3L$
for $5\le T'\le p+1$, size $L$ for $p+2\le T'\le2p+2$, and is
empty above $2p+2$.
The threshold-$A$ list attains the Johnson counting bound exactly.
At the lower threshold
\[
 T=\lceil19p/10\rceil
\]
the complete list of $f$ is still exactly the displayed $N/2$ witnesses,
and the same code satisfies
\[
 N a_1(3/N)<T<\sqrt{2N},
\]
\[
 \frac N2\le\max_{w\in E^D}
 \#\{Q\in E[X]:\deg Q\le2,\ |\{x\in D:w(x)=Q(x)\}|\ge T\}
 \le\frac{4N}{3}.
\]
Thus the lower bound is within a factor $8/3$ of an explicit uniform
finite list bound at the same parameters. Moreover
$T/\sqrt N\to19/(10\sqrt2)$, strictly between the limiting
first-order value $\sqrt{3/2}$ and the Johnson value $\sqrt2$.
Both margins therefore have order $\sqrt N$. Here the rate tends
to zero, $p=\Theta(\sqrt N)$, and $|E|=\Theta(N^{3/2})$; this is not a
fixed-rate or prime-alphabet assertion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The group $G$ has odd order $L$ and lies in the squares of $E^*$, so
$|D|=2L$. For $\operatorname{Norm}(a)=-1$, substitute
$y=a^{p^2}z$ in $y^{p+1}=ay-a^{p^2+1}$. The result is
\[
 z^{p+1}-z+1=0,\qquad z^p=1-1/z.
\]
Neither $0$ nor $1$ is a root. The fractional linear map $1-1/z$
has third iterate the identity, so all roots lie in $E$. The polynomial
is squarefree: its derivative is $z^p-1$, and a common zero would leave
residual $1$. Its $p+1$ roots satisfy
$z^{p^2}=1/(1-z)$ and hence $\operatorname{Norm}(z)=-1$.
Consequently all $p+1$ values of $y$ have norm one. Each has two square
roots in $D$, proving exactly $A$ matches for each of the $L$ distinct
quadratics displayed above.

For any quadratic $Q$, the polynomial $f-Q$ has degree $A$, so no
quadratic has more than $A$ matches. If the complete threshold-$A$ list
has size $m$, let $r_x$ count its matching witnesses at $x$. Distinct
quadratics share at most two matching coordinates. Therefore
\[
 \sum_x r_x=mA,\qquad
 \sum_xr_x^2\le mA+2m(m-1).
\]
Cauchy's inequality gives
\[
 m\le\frac{N(A-2)}{A^2-2N}=L,
 \qquad A^2-2N=4p.
\]
This proves completeness and exact Johnson saturation. Equality also
shows that every coordinate belongs to $p+1$ supports and every pair
of supports shares two coordinates.

We next show that every quadratic outside this bank has at most $p+1$
matches. We use the following elementary semilinear observation. For
$v\ne0$, a nonzero root of $z^{p+1}=uz+v$ satisfies
$z^p=u+v/z$. The threefold Frobenius-twisted composition of this
fractional linear map is the identity exactly when
$u\ne0$ and $v=-u^{1+p^2}$: this follows by multiplying the matrices
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u^{p^i}&v^{p^i}\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
for $i=2,1,0$. Otherwise it has at most two fixed points.
The identity case has exactly $p+1$ roots by the same squarefreeness
and Frobenius argument as above. When $v=0$, there is at most one
nonzero root.

For an even quadratic $Q=aX^2+c$, apply this observation to
$y=x^2$. More than four matches requires
$a\ne0$ and $c=-a^{1+p^2}$. All its $y$-roots have norm
$-\operatorname{Norm}(a)$, so any match on $D$ then requires
$\operatorname{Norm}(a)=-1$.
For $Q=aX^2+bX+c$ with $b\ne0$, put
\[
 r(X)=1-a^pQ(X)-c^pX^2.
\]
At a matching $x\in D$, the norm-one condition gives
$x^2Q(x)^p=1$, hence $r(x)=b^px^{p+2}$. Thus
\[
 r(X)^2-b^{2p}X^2Q(X)
\]
vanishes at every match. If this polynomial is nonzero, there are at
most four matches. If it is zero, $X$ divides $r$ and
\[
 H(X)=\frac{r(X)}{b^pX}
\]
is a polynomial of degree at most one, with $H^2=Q$.
Write $H=uX+v$. Since $b\ne0$, both $u$ and $v$ are nonzero.
Comparing the constant and linear coefficients in $r=b^pXH$ gives
\[
 (u^pv)^2=1,\qquad v^p=-u^{p+1}.
\]
Hence $v=-u^{p^2+1}$ and $\operatorname{Norm}(u)^2=1$.
Conversely these conditions imply the full quartic identity, with
$r/(b^pX)=uX-u^{p^2+1}$.
The original unsquared identity fixes the sign: at every match,
\[
 x^{p+1}=u x-u^{p^2+1}.
\]
This equation has exactly $p+1$ roots in $E$, all with norm
$-\operatorname{Norm}(u)\in\{1,-1\}$, by the semilinear observation.
All lie in $D$ and are matches, so the agreement is exactly $p+1$.
Writing $H_u(X)=uX-u^{p^2+1}$, the $2L$ resulting squares are distinct:
equal squares would give $H_{u'}=\pm H_u$; the minus case forces
$u'=-u$ but has incompatible constant coefficients because $p^2+1$
is even and $p$ is odd.
Their nonzero linear coefficients separate them from the original
even bank. Every remaining quadratic has at most four matches.
This proves the stated complete profile, and in particular the
original bank remains the complete list at $T$.

For the uniform upper bound, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:quadratic-shortening-list}.
Its denominator is positive for $T\ge19p/10$ and $p\ge53$.
By monotonicity it suffices to evaluate $U(N,19p/10)$.
The inequality $U(N,19p/10)\le4N/3$ is equivalent to
\[
 209p^3-10870p^2+1575p+1500\ge0.
\]
After substituting $p=53+u$, the left side is
$209u^3+22361u^2+610598u+666438$, which is positive for $u\ge0$.
The exhibited complete list supplies the lower bound at $T$.
Finally, $T<2p<\sqrt{2N}$, while the first-order curve estimate gives
\[
 N a_1(3/N)\le\sqrt{3N/2}+(3N/8)^{1/4}
 <\frac74p+\sqrt p<T
\]
using $T\ge19p/10$, $\sqrt p>20/3$, and $p\ge53$.
\end{proof}

The matched comparison is codewide, not just a bound along one received
line. Its normalized threshold and normalized separation from the
first-order curve both vanish with the rate; it makes no claim of
constant-factor tightness for exceptional-label or common-agreement
bounds.


\begin{corollary}\label{cor:norm-one-full-interior-band}
With the code and word of Theorem~\ref{thm:norm-one-direct-list}, let
$p\ge53$. At every integer threshold
\[
 \lceil\sqrt3\,p\rceil\le T\le2p+2,
\]
the displayed word has exactly $N/2$ nearby quadratics, and the maximum
list size over all received words is at most $9N/4$.
Consequently, for every fixed $c\in(\sqrt3,2)$, at threshold
$T=\lceil cp\rceil$ the maximum list size is $\Theta(N)$.
For sufficiently large $p$ these thresholds lie strictly between the
first-order curve and the Johnson threshold, with both margins of
order $\sqrt N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The classification in the theorem already gives the exact $N/2$ list
throughout this band. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:quadratic-shortening-list}
and its monotonicity at the real threshold $\sqrt3\,p$.
Its denominator is positive, and $U(N,\sqrt3\,p)\le9N/4$ follows from
\[
 \sqrt3\,p^3-(26\sqrt3+38)p^2+(16-4\sqrt3)p+8>0.
\]
Indeed the coefficient of $p^2$ after extracting it from the first
two terms is $\sqrt3(p-26)-38\ge27\sqrt3-38>0$;
the remaining terms are positive.
Finally, $Na_1(3/N)=\sqrt3\,p+O(\sqrt p)$ and
$\sqrt{2N}=2p+O(1)$. Thus every fixed $c\in(\sqrt3,2)$
has the asserted two strict margins for all sufficiently large $p$.
The finite band in the first statement need not itself lie wholly
between those two boundaries.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}\label{cor:norm-one-general-range}
Fix $\sqrt2<c<2$. For all sufficiently large primes $p$ depending on
$c$, the maximum list size for the norm-one code at
$T=\lceil cp\rceil$ satisfies
\[
 \frac N2\le L_{\max}(T)
 \le\left(\frac{2}{c^2-2}+o_c(1)\right)N.
\]
The upper bound holds on every length-$N$, dimension-three
Reed--Solomon domain over every field. The lower bound uses the same
word and exactly the same $N/2$ witnesses as
Theorem~\ref{thm:norm-one-direct-list}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
With $N=2(p^2+p+1)$ and $T=cp+O(1)$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:quadratic-shortening-list} gives
\[
 \frac{U(N,T)}N=\frac{2}{c^2-2}+O_c(p^{-1}).
\]
Its denominator is positive for all sufficiently large $p$ because
$c^2>2$. Also $p+1<T\le2p+2$ eventually, so the classification
in Theorem~\ref{thm:norm-one-direct-list} gives the exact $N/2$ lower list.
\end{proof}

The ratio of this universal upper bound to the exhibited list tends
to $4/(c^2-2)$. In particular it lies between two and four in the
strict limiting first-order/Johnson interior $\sqrt3<c<2$.
This comparison does not assert that its dependence on $c^2-2$ is
optimal. For $\sqrt2<c\le\sqrt3$ the thresholds are not in that strict
interior. The result concerns growing lists at vanishing rate over
extension fields; it does not supply prime-alphabet tightness or a
bound on exceptional challenges along a received line.
