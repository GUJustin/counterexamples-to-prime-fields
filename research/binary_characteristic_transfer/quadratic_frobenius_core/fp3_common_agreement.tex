\subsection{Native cubic-extension coverage with small common agreement}
\label{sec:fp3-common-agreement}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:fp3-common-agreement}
For every prime $p\ge4099$, there is a dimension-three Reed--Solomon
code on $N=2p^2-p-1$ distinct points of $E=\mathbb F_{p^3}$ and a
received line $f+\lambda g$ with ordinary common agreement at most
$p+2\sqrt p$, such that every $\lambda\in E$ has a nearby codeword at
agreement threshold
\[
 A=2p-\lceil4\sqrt p\rceil-2.
\]
Exactly $p^3-p$ parameters have singleton lists, and the remaining
$p$ parameters each have a list of exactly $p$ codewords.
The threshold satisfies
\[
 N a_1(3/N)<A<\sqrt{2N}.
\]
Thus every parameter is exceptional for ordinary common agreement,
and the singleton fraction is $1-p^{-2}$. The common-agreement gap
is $\Theta(p)=\Theta(\sqrt N)$ and the characteristic exceeds the
message degree. This is an extension-field result at vanishing rate;
the two affine endpoints are themselves nearby.
Equivalently, the endpoints are $f$ and $f+g$, with affine mixtures
$(1-\lambda)f+\lambda(f+g)$; this invertible source change preserves
ordinary common agreement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a two-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-subspace $W\subset E$ and a
square $\eta=s^2\in E\setminus\mathbb F_p$. Put
\[
 D_0=\{x\ne0:x^2\in W\},\qquad
 D_1=\{x\ne0:x^2\in\eta W\}\setminus D_0.
\]
The multiplicative stabilizer of $W$ is $\mathbb F_p^*$: together
with zero it is a subfield of $E$, and it cannot be $E$.
Consequently $W\cap\eta^{-1}W$ is a line; write it as
$\mathbb F_pu_0$. Since $\eta\notin\mathbb F_p$,
\[
 W=u_0\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_p}(1,\eta).
\]
The quadratic character of $E$ restricts to the quadratic character
of $\mathbb F_p$. Each nonzero $\mathbb F_p$-line therefore has
exactly half its elements square in $E$. It follows that
$|D_0|=p^2-1$, the overlap of the two untrimmed blocks has size
$p-1$, and $|D_0\cup D_1|=N$.
Define
\[
 (f,g)=(x^{2p},0)\quad\hbox{on }D_0,\qquad
 (f,g)=\bigl(\eta(x^2/\eta)^p,1\bigr)\quad\hbox{on }D_1.
\]

\emph{Canonical fibers and their sizes.}
For a projective direction $[u]$ in $W$, let
\[
 a_u=u^{p-1},\qquad L_u(y)=y^p-a_uy,\qquad I_u=L_u(W).
\]
The kernel is $\mathbb F_pu$, so $I_u$ is a line. For a basis
$w_1,w_2$ of $W$, put $\delta=w_1w_2^p-w_1^pw_2\ne0$.
The alternating identity $uL_u(y)=uy^p-u^py$ gives
$I_u=(\delta/u)\mathbb F_p$.
The even quadratic $Q=a_uX^2+b$ has canonical fibers on both blocks
precisely when
\[
 b,v\in I_u,\qquad \lambda=b-\eta v.
\]
A zero fiber has exactly $p-1$ physical square-root points.
A nonzero affine fiber $y_0+u\mathbb F_p$ has between
$p-2\sqrt p$ and $p+2\sqrt p$ such points. Indeed,
$y_0/u\notin\mathbb F_p$ has degree three, so
$\operatorname{Norm}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(t+y_0/u)$ is a separable cubic.
The identity between quadratic characters and the norm reduces the
count to $p$ plus a signed quadratic character sum of this cubic.
Hasse's bound for its smooth genus-one double cover bounds that sum
by $2\sqrt p$. Multiplication by the square $\eta$ preserves the counts.

In the normalized fresh variable $y=x^2/\eta$, the deleted overlap
is $\mathbb F_pu_0\setminus\{0\}$. A canonical fresh fiber is
entirely deleted only for $[u]=[u_0]$ and $v=0$; every other fiber
loses at most two physical points. Hence every doubly canonical
candidate except this deleted-fiber case has at least $A$ matches.
The deleted-fiber case has at most $p+2\sqrt p<A$.

\emph{Parameter geometry.}
For nonzero $\lambda$, its representing directions are the projective
points of
\[
 W\cap(\lambda/\delta)^{-1}
          \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_p}(1,\eta).
\]
This intersection has dimension one or two. It has dimension two
exactly when $\lambda\in(\delta/u_0)\mathbb F_p^*$: the stabilizer
calculation above shows there are precisely these $p-1$ scalars.
Thus outside the line $\Lambda_0=(\delta/u_0)\mathbb F_p$, every
parameter has exactly one doubly canonical quadratic. Its fresh
fiber cannot be the deleted one. Each nonzero parameter in
$\Lambda_0$ has all $p+1$ directions; exactly its $u_0$ direction
has $v=0$ and is excluded, leaving $p$ qualifying quadratics.
At zero, all directions have $b=v=0$; again exactly $u_0$ is excluded.
The other $p$ directions give $2p-2\ge A$ matches.

\emph{Excluding all other quadratics.}
First consider one full block $\{x\ne0:x^2\in W\}$ with word $x^{2p}$.
An even quadratic $aX^2+c$ gives $y^p-ay=c$ on $W$. Unless its
kernel direction lies in $W$, this has at most one solution in $W$,
hence at most two physical matches. If its kernel direction lies in
$W$, its nonempty fibers are exactly the canonical ones above.
In particular every even quadratic has at most $p+2\sqrt p$ matches.

We claim every non-even quadratic has at most eight matches on this
block. Write $Q=aX^2+bX+c$, $b\ne0$. If $Q=(uX+w)^2$ over $E$,
then $u,w\ne0$ and the two Frobenius equations are
$x^p=\pm(ux+w)$. Since
$\operatorname{Norm}(-u)=-\operatorname{Norm}(u)$, at most one has
an affine $\mathbb F_p$-line of solutions; the other has at most one.
The affine line cannot contain zero. Write it as $z+t v$ with
$z/v\notin\mathbb F_p$. If more than two of its points had squares
in $W$, projecting $(z+tv)^2$ to the one-dimensional quotient $E/W$
would give an identically zero quadratic in $t$. This would put
$z^2,zv,v^2$ in $W$, impossible because
$1,z/v,(z/v)^2$ are independent over $\mathbb F_p$.
Thus the square case has at most three matches.

If $Q$ is not a square in $E[X]$, put
$a_i=a^{p^i},b_i=b^{p^i},c_i=c^{p^i}$ and
\[
 R=X^2-a_2a_1Q-a_2c_1-c_2,\qquad d=a_2b_1,
\]
\[
 F=R^2+(d^2-b_2^2a_1)Q-b_2^2c_1,\qquad
 G=-2dR-b_2^2b_1.
\]
At a match, writing $y=x^p,z=x^{p^2}$ and using
$y^2=Q(x)$, $z^2=Q^{(p)}(y)$, $x^2=Q^{(p^2)}(z)$ gives
$b_2z=R(x)-dy$ and then $F(x)+G(x)y=0$.
Therefore the match is a root of $F^2-G^2Q$, of degree at most eight.
This polynomial is nonzero. Otherwise nonsquareness of $Q$ forces
$F=G=0$. If $a=0$, however, $G=-b_2^2b_1\ne0$; and if $a\ne0$,
$G=0$ forces $R$ constant, contrary to its nonzero linear coefficient
$-a_2a_1b$. This proves the claim.

The same classification applies on the second block after $x=st$
and division by $\eta$, with the constant shifted by $\lambda$.
Non-even quadratics therefore have at most sixteen active matches.
An even quadratic canonical on only one block has zero matches on
the other and at most $p+2\sqrt p$ in all; an even quadratic canonical
on neither has at most four. Since $A>\max(16,p+2\sqrt p)$, the
parameter geometry lists every qualifying codeword, proving the exact
list counts.

\emph{Ordinary common agreement.}
A direction quadratic other than the constants zero and one matches
$g$ on at most four points. Direction zero restricts simultaneous
agreements to $D_0$, and direction one to $D_1$. The one-block
classification bounds either by $\max(p+2\sqrt p,8)=p+2\sqrt p$.
This proves the common-agreement bound. The endpoints $f$ and $f+g$
are themselves nearby, as follows already from the all-parameter claim.

\emph{Thresholds and finite applicability.}
The stated onset implies $A\ge2p-5\sqrt p$ and
\[
 N a_1(3/N)\le\sqrt{3N/2}+(3N/8)^{1/4}
 <\tfrac74p+\sqrt p<A.
\]
Also $A<2p-1<\sqrt{2N}$ and $A>\max(16,p+2\sqrt p)$.
One can certify first-order interpolation directly using the
derivative-weighted support of \cite[Proposition~5.10]{dkt2026}:
choose multiplicity six, derivative cap three, total jet cap $B=3A$,
and challenge height $H=40B$. Its coefficient count is $G=4B^2-2B$
and its local-rank bound is $62$. For $p\ge4099$,
$29p/5\le B\le6p$, and
\[
 39G-40\cdot62N\ge\frac{7196}{25}p^2-468p>0.
\]
Since $G>62N$, this makes
$(H-B+1)G-(H+1)62N>0$, a sufficient strict graded row test.
The reconstruction guard is only $p>3$.
This certifies applicability, not a matching numerical exceptional-count
upper bound. The rate and normalized common-agreement gap vanish.
\end{proof}

An exact physical-fiber census independently checks the list spectrum
at $p=47$ and $p=53$: respectively, there are $103776$ singleton
parameters and $47$ lists of size $47$, and $148824$ singleton
parameters and $53$ lists of size $53$.
These checks validate the construction and counts at the stated
thresholds; the uniform finite-certificate onset above remains $p\ge4099$.
The replay scripts, separate quadratic-root proof, and independent
certificate audit accompany the source.
