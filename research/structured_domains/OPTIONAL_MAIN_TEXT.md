# Optional main-text paragraph

The interval certificates do not transfer merely by replacing an index
$i$ with a subgroup element $g^i$. For $n\ge 2^{m+1}$, the positive and
negative coefficient supports of
$Q(X)=\prod_{r=0}^{m}(1-X^{2^r})$ have equal integer moments through
degree $m$: $Q$ has a root of multiplicity $m+1$ at $1$. Yet their
first field moments differ by $Q(g)\ne0$ when $g$ has order $n$.
Nor does counting all field-valued signatures recover a large
below-Elias list certificate. For $p>n$, $t=k+m<n$, and $k,m\ge1$,
that argument gives $\lceil\binom nt/p^m\rceil$. If
$\theta=1-t/n$ is strictly below the characteristic-$p$ Elias radius
at rate $k/n$, the entropy inequality implies
$\binom nt/p^m<(p/(p-1))^{n\theta}<e$, so this guarantee is at most
three. A substantial certificate on a prescribed subgroup therefore
requires additional information about its actual coefficient fibers.
These limitations concern the two transfer arguments, not the true
list size.
