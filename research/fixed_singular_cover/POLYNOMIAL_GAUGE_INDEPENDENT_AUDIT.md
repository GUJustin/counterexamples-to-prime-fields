# Independent polynomial-gauge corollary audit

September 18, 2026. **PASS** for `cor:riccati-polynomial-gauge` as currently stated in `appendix.tex`. This is an independent proof audit, not an exhaustive computational check. No theorem or manuscript edit was required.

Write `a=deg_X B`, `E=D+a`, and retain the surrounding assumptions `D<A<=n` and a distinct evaluation domain. The corollary assumes characteristic zero or greater than `E`, the polynomial divisibility `B | T B_X`, and fixed bounded challenge degree `h`.

1. **Gauge identity and degrees.** Multiplying the original identity by `B` and using `Y=BP` gives exactly `T Y' - Y^2 +(R-T B_X/B)Y-BS=0`. The divisibility assumption is needed in the polynomial ring in both variables. The quotient has challenge degree at most `2h` (or is zero), and `BS` also has challenge degree at most `2h`; thus the transformed coefficient bound remains a constant depending only on `h`. Candidate degree is at most `E`. At a label where `B(X,z)` is not the zero polynomial, multiplication by it is injective on actual candidates. Labels where it is identically zero lie among roots of one nonzero challenge coefficient, so number at most `h`.

2. **Low derivative degree.** If `deg_X T<=2E`, apply the existing singular-cover theorem directly to the original candidates, not to a transformed notion of badness. Their separant is `T`. The persistent singular set has size at most `2E`, and outside it there are at most `hn` singular coordinate-label incidences. Take `e=floor(lambda n/2)`; at most `2h/lambda` labels have more than `e` such incidences. The cover's threshold is at most `(4E+D)/3=(5D+4a)/3`, so its remaining nonsingular budget has linear slack. Also `A-D>=lambda n`. These are exactly the conditions needed for its linear conclusion.

3. **High derivative degree.** If `deg_X T>2E`, the preceding monic theorem's recurrence and resonant plane-elimination constructions apply with message degree `E` and challenge bound at most `2h`. Nonresonant rational families, excluded labels, and isolated points have complexity `O_h(E)`; the resonant curve has bounded degree (at most four) in its message parameter and `O_h(E)` degree in the challenge. The transformed agreement equation is `Y(x)=B(x,z)(f(x)+zg(x))`, so its challenge degree changes by only a bounded additive amount. The same intersection calculation therefore gives `O_h(E)` nonpersistent incidences per coordinate. This argument uses equations and incidences, not the monic theorem's conclusion about bad transformed supports.

4. **Persistent coordinates and original badness.** Remove coordinates for which `B(x,z)` is identically zero; a nonzero challenge coefficient of `B` bounds their number by `a`. On a source curve with at most `D` persistent remaining coordinates, an actual candidate with `A` original matches contributes at least `A-a-D` nonpersistent incidences. The hypothesis gives `A-a-D >= (2D+a)/3+lambda n >=lambda n`. It also gives `E<=3n/4`, hence `E=O(n)`. Thus the incidence bound yields only `O_{h,lambda}(n)` labels on such curves.

5. **Rich persistent curves.** On a nonvertical source curve with more than `D` persistent remaining coordinates, fix `D+1` of them. Their `B(x,z)` values are nonzero polynomials in `z`; exclude at most `h(D+1)` roots. Division then shows that every ACTUAL original candidate on the curve agrees with the affine received line at these same `D+1` coordinates. Interpolation places those original candidates on one affine codeword pencil. A bad full support must contain an accidental coordinate of that pencil, and there are at most `n` associated labels. The number of source curves is bounded by their message degree; vertical components are already covered by recurrence exclusions. This treats original full-support badness without assuming all transformed candidates are divisible by `B` or that transformed and original supports coincide.

Consequently the stated `O_{h,lambda}(n)` bound is justified in both cases. The characteristic guard through `D+a`, the divisibility condition, and the actual-candidate restriction are essential parts of its scope. This corollary does not cover arbitrary nonconstant quadratic coefficients without those guards.
