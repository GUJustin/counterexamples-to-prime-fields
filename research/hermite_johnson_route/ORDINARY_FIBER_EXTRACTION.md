# What normalization can remove an interpolation-imposed ordinary fiber?

The raw source's full ordinary core cannot in general be removed by dividing out a domain factor, taking a squarefree part, or taking its first nonzero contact block. There is, however, an exact global normal form describing the necessary normalization. It identifies a valid conditional content removal and explains why the general operation introduces higher jets.

## 1. Ordinary-fiber ideal

Let the distinct domain coordinates be x_1,...,x_n, let `Λ(X)=∏(X−x_i)`, and let F,G be the degree-<n interpolants of the received value and direction. Write `W0(X,z)=F(X)+zG(X)`.

A polynomial Q(X,Y,R,z) vanishes identically in R,z on every received fiber if and only if

`Q ∈ (Λ(X),Y−W0(X,z))`.

Proof: divide Q by the monic linear polynomial Y−W0. Its remainder is Q(X,W0,R,z). Each of its R,z coefficients vanishes at every domain coordinate, equivalently is divisible by Λ. Thus Q has the exact decomposition

`Q=(Y−W0)A+Λ B`.

This does **not** imply that Λ divides Q. The first term is the essential obstruction to naive domain-factor removal.

## 2. Exact normal form for the full contact source

Choose Hermite lifts W(X,z),E(X), of X-degree less than 2n, with

`W(x_i,z)=f_i+zg_i`, `W_X(x_i,z)=0`,
`E(x_i)=0`, `E'(x_i)=1`.

Here W remains affine in z. These lifts exist over the original field by the Chinese remainder theorem modulo the pairwise comaximal ideals `(X−x_i)^2`; no factorial division is required. Put

`V=Y−W(X,z)−E(X)R`.

For multiplicity m≥1, the current source's local contact conditions are equivalent to the normal form

`Q=Σ_b Λ(X)^max(m−2b,0) H_b(X,R,z) V^b`.             (1)

To verify this, at x write T=X−x and `U=Y−w_x(z)−T R`. Under the source homogenized substitution `Y=w_x(z)+T a`, one has U=T(a−R). The condition that the r-th T coefficient is divisible by `(a−R)^max(m−r,0)` is exactly membership in the weighted ideal generated by `T^e U^b` with `e+2b≥m`.

Locally, `Λ=T*unit` and `V=U+T² C(T,R,z)`. Replacing U by V preserves this weighted ideal (both directions follow from the inverse substitution). Expand Q uniquely in powers of the monic variable V. Its b-th coefficient must be divisible at x by `T^max(m−2b,0)`. Imposing this at all distinct coordinates gives the divisibility by the corresponding power of Λ in (1). Conversely (1) satisfies every local condition. The unit assertion is understood modulo the relevant finite power of T, which suffices for these polynomial divisibility tests.

### Safe conditional extraction

If `deg_Y Q≤t` and `m>2t`, (1) forces the genuine global polynomial factor

`Λ^(m−2t) | Q`.

Dividing by this factor preserves **every** actual polynomial solution of `Q(X,P,P',z)=0`, since a nonzero polynomial in X is not a zero divisor. It does not increase derivative degree. More generally, dividing the gcd in F[X] of all Y,R,z coefficients is always safe, and squarefree factor reduction preserves the union of solution sets.

The consequence is also a useful necessary condition: an X-primitive nonzero multiplicity-m source must have `deg_Y Q≥ceil(m/2)`. The current automatic Y cap is much larger than m/2, so this does not force removable content in the benchmark sources. A bounded **derivative** degree is not a bounded Y-degree and does not change that conclusion.

Neither this content removal nor (1) guarantees a controlled ordinary core afterwards. Establishing that property of the resulting identity is a separate theorem, not a formal cancellation.

## 3. An actual-source obstruction to factor-only normalization

Take any distinct domain in characteristic different from two, choose the Hermite lift E above, and let the received line be `w_x(z)=x+z`. Consider

`Q(X,Y,R,z)=Y−X−z+E(X)(1−R)`.

At every domain coordinate, Q(x,x+z,R,z)=0 identically. Its zeroth block vanishes, and its first block is `a−R`; hence it obeys the actual multiplicity-two contact conditions. Every degree-one polynomial `P_z(X)=X+z` is an actual solution.

This polynomial is irreducible, because it is monic linear in Y, and its coefficient gcd in F[X] is one. Its derivative degree is only one. Thus neither X-content division, squarefree reduction, nor selection of a nonconstant factor changes its full ordinary core. It is also compatible with the source contact-degree support at code degree one, full agreement A=n, multiplicity m=2: `deg_X E<2n=mA`, Y has weight one, and R has weight zero. No claim that this example gives bad labels is made; its solutions form an affine pencil.

The identity R−1 also holds on the displayed pencil, but it is **not** obtained by dividing Q. Deriving such a new common identity would require additional knowledge of the actual polynomial solution set. This example therefore rules out the proposed universal factor-only normalization, not every possible solution-set elimination method.

## 4. Prolongation explains the missing higher-jet information

At an ordinary fiber, Q_R(x,w_x(z),v,z)=0 identically. Consequently an actual solution agreeing there obeys the first local prolongation equation

`Q_X(x,w_x(z),v,z)+v Q_Y(x,w_x(z),v,z)=0`,

where v=P'(x). This is a valid conditional lemma: if this polynomial is nonzero and has a unique rational root, it provides a first-jet prescription without changing the global identity. Its derivative degree is at most one more than that of Q.

For the current contact source with m≥2, however, the r=1 contact condition makes this polynomial identically zero as well: substitute a=R into its block `Q_X+a Q_Y`. Thus the immediate prolongation extracts no information.

More generally, along an actual polynomial matching the value at x,

`a(T)=(P(x+T)−P(x))/T`, `R(T)=P'(x+T)`,

and

`a(T)−R(T)=−P^[2](x) T+O(T²)`.

Here P^[2] is the second Hasse coefficient. The first nonzero contact block contains powers of a−R; removing them therefore exposes the second and potentially higher jets, rather than automatically producing another first-order identity. For the explicit example above, the second derivative of Q(X,P,P',z)=0 at a domain coordinate gives

`P''(x)=−E''(x)(P'(x)−1)`.

This already involves P''. Treating a−R as a nonzero global factor and canceling it would lose legitimate solutions and is not justified.

## Conclusion and exact remaining task

A legitimate first-order normalization must either (i) identify genuine global content/factors and then prove the remaining ordinary core small, or (ii) derive a new bounded-degree identity valid on the entire actual solution set by a separate elimination theorem. Local first-nonzero contact blocks alone do not supply (ii). The normal form (1) is an exact structural description, but no bounded-degree extraction theorem or new benchmark certificate is claimed.
