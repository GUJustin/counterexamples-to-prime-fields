\subsubsection{Sampling the code and line without enumerating a moment class}
\label{sec:cubic-sampling}

The large moment class and the largest evaluation images need not be
found explicitly. Random choices produce the code and received line
with a quantitative success guarantee.

\begin{proposition}[A direct randomized construction]
\label{cw:sampling}
Use the parameters of Lemma~\ref{cw:finite}, with seed
$\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$ and anchor zero. Put
\[
 Q=\binom{m-1}{t-1},\qquad
 \mu=Q/\mathcal V,
\]
where $\mathcal V$ is an upper bound on the number of first-$3s$
integer binomial-moment vectors of the anchored supports. Choose an
anchored support uniformly, an injective cubic map uniformly, $q$
distinct outside coordinates uniformly, and independent uniform
nonzero padding directions. Let $w$ be the locator of the chosen
transformed support after removing its anchor, and output $w+zg$.
For every $L_*\ge2$ and $\gamma>0$, the probability that some nonzero
parameter is not nearby is at most
\begin{equation}\label{cw:sampling-failure}
 \frac{L_*}{\mu}+\frac{\delta}{\gamma}
 +U_0\left(\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\right)^q,
 \qquad
 \delta=\frac{U_0}{L_*}+\frac{U_0B}{R}-1.
\end{equation}
Every output has the exact far-point distance and absence of correlated
agreement in Lemma~\ref{cw:finite}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The chance that the sampled support belongs to a moment class of size
less than $L_*$ is at most $\mathcal V L_*/Q=L_*/\mu$, by summing
the sizes of such classes. Equal binomial moments imply equal power
sums through the same order. Condition on any class of size $L\ge L_*$.
For an injective map, let $r_{x,v}$ be the multiplicity of the nonzero
difference value $v=P_i(x)-w(x)$, and put
\[
 E=\sum_{x\text{ outside}}\sum_{v\ne0}r_{x,v}^2,
 \qquad \Delta=\frac{U_0E}{RL^2}-1.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\Delta\ge0$. The collision average proved in
Lemma~\ref{cw:finite} gives
\[
 \mathbb E E\le LR+L(L-1)B,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\Delta\le\frac{U_0}{L}
       +\frac{U_0(L-1)B}{RL}-1\le\delta.
\]
Hence $\Pr(\Delta>\gamma)\le\delta/\gamma$.
If $\Delta\le\gamma$, the average outside image size is at least
$L^2R/E\ge U_0/(1+\gamma)$.

For sampling distinct coordinates, the mean product of their
nonnegative missing fractions is at most the corresponding power of
the population mean. Indeed, replacing two entries by their average
increases each elementary symmetric mean: with all other entries fixed,
the only changing term is a nonnegative multiple of their product.
Iterating this averaging proves the inequality. The expected number
of uncovered labels is therefore at most
$U_0(\gamma/(1+\gamma))^q$, and the probability of any uncovered label
is at most this expectation. Combining the three bad events proves
\eqref{cw:sampling-failure}. The degree of $w$ and its selected roots
give the far-point claim for every output, independently of the
coverage event.
\end{proof}

For the parameters in Theorem~\ref{cw:punctured-line}, the elementary
moment ranges give $\mu\ge p^{1+\epsilon}$ for a fixed $\epsilon>0$.
Taking $L_*=p^{1+\epsilon/2}$ in the analysis gives
$\delta=p^{-\Omega(1)}$. Since $q=\Theta(\log p)$, a sufficiently small
fixed $\gamma$ makes the last term in \eqref{cw:sampling-failure}
$p^{-\Omega(1)}$ as well. Thus the algorithm succeeds with probability
$1-p^{-\Omega(1)}$. It does not need to compute $L_*$, the moment class,
or any image size. Rejection sampling makes the cubic map uniform
among injective maps in expected $O(1)$ trials. Forming and evaluating
$w$ takes $O(n^2)$ field operations, so the code and line are output
in expected polynomial time in $\log p$. This does not give a procedure
for recovering a nearby codeword at a supplied nonzero parameter.

For an exact finite example, take $p=2^{521}-1$,
$(n,K,m,t)=(990,495,900,497)$, $s=1$, $L_*=p2^{150}$, and
$\gamma=2^{-8}$. The exact bound in \eqref{cw:sampling-failure} is
less than $2^{-137}$. An independent integer replay bounds each of
its three terms by $2^{-130}$, and hence their sum by $2^{-128}$.
The radius is strictly below Elias, the far point is $\eta/2$ outside,
and the numerical prescription fraction is below $2^{-16}$.
The programs \path{generate_sample.py} and \path{verify_sample.py} in
\path{research/cubic_domain_warp/} output and replay a stored sample.
Its domain, line values, and exact far-point properties are checked
deterministically. Complete coverage has the stated probability guarantee
over the generator's randomness; it is not deterministically certified
for the individual stored sample.
