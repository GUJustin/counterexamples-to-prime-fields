## Why a naive dyadic induction does not yet prove a strong bound

Write k=2s, Y=X², P(X)=E(Y)+XO(Y), with deg E,O≤s−1. The word descends to W_s(Y). If both signs above Y match, then O(Y)=0. Hence a non-descended candidate has at most s−1 full opposite pairs. However the remaining singleton matches are unconstrained by this observation; it does not give M≤k+O(1).

The exact norm polynomial (W_s−E)²−YO² has degree2k. Each full matched fiber contributes multiplicity≥2 and each singleton at least1, so its root count merely gives M≤2k. Reducing it modulo Y^(2k)−1 changes derivatives at full fibers: multiplicity cannot be carried through that reduction. Thus the tempting reduced-degree improvement is invalid.

An exact dyadic algebraic target remains available. For any k+1 support roots S={ζ^a}, existence of an agreeing degree<k candidate is equivalent to h_k(S)=2. Write H_S(Z)=h_k(Z^a:a∈S). For k a power of2, Φ_(4k)=Z^(2k)+1; therefore the criterion is the integer coefficient identity H_S(Z)≡2 modulo Z^(2k)+1. Reducing modulo2 gives the necessary condition H_S divisible by (Z+1)^(2k). This condition can vanish on structured supports (e.g. k2 support exponents0,2,4), so reduction at2 alone is not an exclusion. A theorem needs higher 2-adic information or a structural classification of these vanishing supports. No such classification is claimed here.
