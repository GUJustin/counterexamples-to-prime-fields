# Basic ruled-surface log numbers do not obstruct the saturated target

For L=8 sextic graphs on F_6 with28 ordinary quadruple points and no other
intersections, let D be their union. Write S=C0+6f, K=-2C0-8f.
The pairwise root saturation ensures all intersections are transverse and the
distinct leading coefficients ensure no intersections at infinity.

Then (K+D)^2=264. Blowing up each ordinary quadruple point and including the
exceptional curves in the reduced boundary subtracts(4-2)^2=4 per point.
Thus log c1^2=152. The complement Euler number is
4-8*2+28*(4-1)=72. In particular152<3*72; the elementary log Miyaoka-Yau
comparison supplies no contradiction.

More generally the proposed L-word target has section degree e=L-2,
4(L-1) ordinary points of multiplicity L/2, and no remaining pair intersections.
For even L at least6, the direct intersection calculation gives

(K+L(C0+e f))^2 = e L(L-2)-4(L-2),
log c1^2 = e L(L-2)-4(L-2)-4(L-1)(L/2-2)^2,
log c2 = 4-2L+4(L-1)(L/2-1).

With e=L-2 these simplify to log c1^2=5L^2-24L+24 and
log c2=2(L-2)^2. For L8 these are152 and72. Their asymptotic ratio is5/2,
below3; indeed3 log c2-log c1^2=L^2.
This is only a diagnostic against this particular numerical obstruction; it
neither constructs the arrangements nor rules out stronger inequalities.

Literature entry point: Giancarlo Urzua, Arrangements of rational sections over
curves and the varieties they define, https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.4928.
The calculations above are direct intersection and Euler-number calculations,
not an invocation of an unverified stronger theorem from that paper.
