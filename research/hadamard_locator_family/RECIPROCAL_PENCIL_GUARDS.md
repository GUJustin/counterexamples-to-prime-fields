# Guards on the reciprocal-pair pencil

For the sufficient pencil h_g=H+K epsilon_g m_g, with epsilon=(1,-1,-1,1),
write the paired roots as r_g,+ = m_g+n_g and r_g,- = m_g-n_g.
Their product is h_g and their sum is2m_g. Therefore

(r_g,+ - K epsilon_g/2)(r_g,- - K epsilon_g/2)=H+K^2/4.

If the right side is zero, each of the four pairs has a root equal to
K epsilon_g/2. These four roots all have square K^2/4. Thus the required
pairwise distinct eight root squares exclude this entire chart (including K=0).
Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two, the
right side can consequently be normalized to1 by a common nonzero scaling.
Every point of this guarded pencil then has the form

r_g,+ = epsilon_g c+u_g, r_g,- = epsilon_g c+u_g^{-1},

with all u_g nonzero. Also K=0 makes every h_g=H; all three edge squares among
the first three selector rows equal -H. Hence the distinct-edge guard forces
K nonzero, and therefore c nonzero after scaling.

This removes the zero-normalization chart only WITHIN this pencil. It does not
assert that every independent-selector solution belongs to the pencil, nor does
it establish a valid construction. Remaining three selector equations and all
node/leading-coefficient guards must still be satisfied.

## Later correction to the promise of this pencil

The pencil itself is not a viable family: each old edge square among the first
three selector rows equals -H. For example, for the agreeing pair groups0,1,
e3/2=m0(H-Km1)+m1(H+Km0)=H(m0+m1)=H e1/2.
The other two pairs have the same cancellation. Thus even K nonzero does not
remove the three-edge collision. The remaining task is the exceptional rank
stratum of the three-row coefficient matrix, not solving the reciprocal family.
This observation supersedes treating this pencil as a promising positive family.
