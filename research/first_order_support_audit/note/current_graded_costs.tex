\section{Necessary costs of the current monomial-support bounds}
\label{sec:current-graded}

We now use the current ePrint's graded challenge count and squarefree
reconstruction formulas~\cite[Equations (54)--(58), (60), (63)--(64)]{DKTcurrent}.
For monomial supports with full derivative-weighted coefficient prefixes,
their
quadratic list-budget and fourth-power regular-MCA-budget dependence
cannot be improved by choosing a different such support or a smaller
height certified by either the graded row or column-only test on supports
downward in $Y_0$. This is a statement about
these numerical bounds, not actual component degrees, lists or exceptions.
The support restriction below is essential.

\begin{theorem}[Current downward-support costs]\label{thm:current-graded}
Let $N\ge12$ be divisible by four, $D=N/4-1$, and let $D+1<A\le N$
be an integer. Let $S$ be a nonempty finite monomial support downward in $Y_0$,
let $m\ge1$, $B=\max_S(u+v)$ and $b=\max_S v$, and use all monomials
\[
 X^xY_0^uY_1^v,\qquad
 (u,v)\in S,\quad x+Du+(D-1)v<mA,
 \qquad x,u,v\ge0.
\]
Assume characteristic zero or greater than every retained total jet degree.
Write $G_q,R_q$ for the exact source dimension and local rank on total jet
degree $q$, and put
\[
 a_* =\frac{AN-8}{N(N-8)},\qquad
 \varepsilon_*=a_*-a_0,\qquad c_0=\frac{1-2a_0}{8}.
\]
Suppose $0<\varepsilon_*\le1/2000$. If an integer $H\ge0$ passes the
following graded row test for an affine received line:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-graded-test}
 \sum_q(H-q+1)_+(G_q-NR_q)>0.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-graded-costs}
 m>\frac{c_0}{\varepsilon_*},\qquad B>\frac m4,\qquad
 b\ge\frac m8,\qquad
 H+1>\frac{m}{36864\varepsilon_*},\qquad
 H>\frac{m}{73728\varepsilon_*}.
\end{equation}
Alternatively, if $G_u^{(0)}$ counts source coefficients of $Y_0$ exponent $u$
and $G=\sum_uG_u^{(0)}$, $R=\sum_qR_q$, the column test
\[
 \sum_u(H-u+1)_+G_u^{(0)}>NR(H+1)
\]
gives the same bounds on $m,B,b$, and
\[
 H+1>\frac{m}{589824\varepsilon_*},\qquad
 H>\frac{m}{1179648\varepsilon_*}.
\]
Under either test, the current squarefree list budget is therefore
$\Omega(D\varepsilon_*^{-2})$, and its regular-family MCA budget is
$\Omega(D^2\varepsilon_*^{-4})$, even after optimizing the latter's
integer incidence threshold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first reduce positive row-test feasibility to a coordinatewise order
ideal, allowing deletions as well as sorting. Delete degrees $q>H$ and
final diagonals $L_q<m$; their contributions are respectively zero and
nonpositive. The remaining support is saturated and all its degrees are
active. Maintain a decreasing suffix of column heights, all no larger
than the remaining prefix. Choose a minimum height $c$ at column $i$ of
that prefix, whose last column is $j$. If $i=j$, fix it. Otherwise let
$E$ be the excess above $c$ in columns $i+1,\ldots,j$, and let $T=S\setminus E$
be the unchanged baseline.

For the excess, let $g_q$ be its normalized coefficient count, and let
$d_q$ be its marginal local rank relative to $T$, both in total degree $q$.
Its contribution is $\sum_q(H-q+1)(g_q-d_q)$. If this is nonpositive,
delete $E$ and fix column $j$ at height $c$. Otherwise truncate only $E$
at a degree maximizing its prefix surplus
$\sum_{q\le k}(g_q-d_q)$. This weakly increases its weighted contribution
and makes its total marginal surplus positive. Each trimmed column remains
an initial interval above $c$, so the baseline and the minimum height persist.

Move the trimmed excess one column left. For a local grade $\ell$, write
$C(q,\ell)$ for the baseline active count and $X(q,\ell)$ for the excess
active count. The baseline is a height-$c$ order ideal together with excess
intervals in columns $v<i$. Its active diagonal count is nonincreasing at
$q\ge i+c$: the order-ideal part has passed its first incomplete diagonal,
and every earlier excess interval starts by $i+c-1$. Shifted excess occurs
only in this range. Concavity of $f_\ell(z)=\min(z,m-\ell)$ therefore gives
\[
 f_\ell(C(q,\ell)+X(q+1,\ell))-f_\ell(C(q,\ell))
 \le f_\ell(C(q+1,\ell)+X(q+1,\ell))-f_\ell(C(q+1,\ell)),
\]
so the shifted marginal ranks satisfy $d'_q\le d_{q+1}$. Every shifted
coefficient prefix grows by $D-1$ and its challenge weight grows by one.
The complete normalized margin consequently increases by at least
\[
 \sum_q(g_q-d_q)+\frac{D-1}{N}
       \sum_{(u,v)\in E}(H-u-v+2)>0.
\]
The new last prefix column has height $c$, and all other prefix heights
remain at least $c$. Thus each step fixes another column of the decreasing
suffix. The process terminates at an order ideal, with the same $H$ and
no larger degree caps. Saturation persists because degrees only decrease.
This is positive-feasibility preservation after deletion, not monotonicity
of the full decreasing rearrangement of an arbitrary support.

For the resulting order ideal, normalize the prefix surpluses by $N$:
\[
 P_j=\sum_{q\le j}(G_q/N-R_q).
\]
The left side of \eqref{eq:current-graded-test}, divided by $N$, is
$\sum_{j=0}^H P_j$. Some prefix is therefore positive. Total-degree
truncation preserves the full coefficient prefixes and downward-$Y_0$
closure. Applying the exact finite-length parameter-cost corollary of
Section~\ref{sec:gap-costs} to that prefix gives
$m>c_0/\varepsilon_*$, maximum $Y_0$ degree greater than $m/4$, and
maximum $Y_1$ degree greater than $m/4-1$. Hence $m\ge16$,
$B>m/4$, $b\ge m/8$, and $H>m/4-1\ge m/8$.

For every prefix, whether positive or negative, the reduction in
Section~\ref{sec:finite-length} deletes final diagonals of nonpositive
surplus and produces a saturated support $S_0$. Its exact coefficient
benefit is dominated by the leading benefit at $a_*$, with every retained
benefit positive. Thus each retained total degree is less than
$4ma_*<2m$, and $|S_0|\le2m^2+m\le3m^2$. The critical support converse
at $a_0$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:graded-prefix-upper}
 P_j\le B_{m,1/4,a_*}(S_0)-r_m(S_0)
 \le m\varepsilon_*|S_0|\le3\varepsilon_*m^3.
\end{equation}
The middle inequality remains valid if some benefits at $a_0$ are
nonpositive, by deleting those monomials. Empty $S_0$ causes no problem.

To force a triangle in the original support, apply the preceding finite
reduction to a positive prefix. Its retained order ideal has positive
leading surplus. If $d$ is its first incomplete diagonal, every diagonal
has length at most $d$, by the upward-shadow argument in
Lemma~\ref{lem:lex}. Diagonal compression preserves leading benefit and
weakly decreases rank, and has derivative degree at most $d-1$. The
positive-surplus derivative-degree bound therefore gives $d>m/4$.
Consequently, putting $J=\lfloor m/8\rfloor$, the original support contains
all $q+1$ jet monomials on every degree $q\le J$. Their coefficient prefixes are
nonempty and locally saturated, since
\[
 L_q=mA-(D-1)q\ge m\bigl(A-(D-1)/8\bigr)\ge m.
\]
The exact rank formula, restricted to $\ell=q,\ldots,m-q-1$, gives
\[
 R_q=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\min\{\min(q,\ell)+1,m-\ell\}
 \ge(m-2q)(q+1)\ge\frac{3m(q+1)}4.
\]
The exact coefficient comparison gives $G_q/N\le ma_*(q+1)$.
As $a_*<47/100$, we obtain
\[
 G_q/N-R_q\le-\frac{m(q+1)}4,
 \qquad P_j\le-\frac{m(j+1)(j+2)}8\quad(0\le j\le J).
\]
Since $H\ge J$, the negative prefix area in the graded sum is at least
\[
 -\sum_{j=0}^J P_j\ge\frac{m(J+1)(J+2)(J+3)}{24}
 >\frac{m^4}{12288}.
\]
Every positive prefix is at most $3\varepsilon_*m^3$ by
\eqref{eq:graded-prefix-upper}. Positivity of the entire sum implies
$3\varepsilon_*m^3(H+1)>m^4/12288$, proving the $H+1$ bound.
Since $H\ge m/8\ge2$, also $H\ge(H+1)/2$, proving the last inequality
in \eqref{eq:current-graded-costs}.

For the column test, first reduce a merely downward-$Y_0$ support to an
order ideal without increasing $H,B,b$. Write $h=H+1$ and
$w_u=(h-u)_+$. A final diagonal with $L_q<m$ contributes at most
$h(G_q-NR_q)\le0$ to the column margin, by the finite half-rank lemma.
Delete these diagonals. The retained local rank is saturated, and the
retained jet support still has column heights $h_v$.
Its exact weighted coefficient contribution in column $v$ is
\[
 \alpha(h_v)-(D-1)v\beta(h_v),\qquad
 \alpha(d)=\sum_{u<d}w_u(mA-Du),\quad
 \beta(d)=\sum_{u<d}w_u.
\]
Since $\beta$ is nondecreasing, sorting the heights in decreasing order
increases this coefficient sum by rearrangement. The height-sorting
lemma weakly decreases the saturated local rank. Sorting respects every
decreasing upper bound on column heights: otherwise the first violating
sorted height would require more large original columns than the bound
allows. In particular it preserves the total-degree cutoff used for
saturation, the original total-degree cap $B$, and positive coefficient
prefixes; the derivative cap $b$ cannot increase. Thus the same column
test holds for the sorted order ideal, with no larger declared caps.

For this order ideal, put $\Delta=G/N-R$. The column test is exactly
\[
 (H+1)\Delta>\sum_u\min(u,H+1)G_u^{(0)}/N.
\]
Thus $\Delta>0$; the same mandatory-triangle argument applies to the full
support, and $\Delta\le3\varepsilon_*m^3$. The support truncated to
$u\le H$ also has positive surplus: its source dimension exceeds $NR$,
and its rank is at most $R$. The finite converse gives $H>m/4$.
Every triangle monomial has at least $Nm/8$ coefficient monomials, since
$(mA-Du-(D-1)v)/N\ge(mA-Dm/8)/N\ge m/8$. Hence, using $J\ge m/16$,
\[
 \sum_u\min(u,H+1)G_u^{(0)}/N
 \ge\frac m8\frac{J(J+1)(J+2)}6
 \ge\frac{m^4}{196608}.
\]
This proves the two stated column-test height bounds.

For the current reconstruction formulas, write
\[
 \tau=2D-3,\quad u=1+\tau(B-1),\quad
 v=\min\{u,\tau(b-1)+D\},\quad F=Bv+b(u-v).
\]
We have $D\ge2$, $B\ge2$, $0\le v\le u$ and $\tau\ge D/2$. Thus
\[
 F=bu+(B-b)v\ge bu\ge\frac{DBb}{4}
 >\frac{Dm^2}{128}.
\]
The squarefree list budget is $\lambda_{\rm agr}F+S$, where
$\lambda_{\rm agr}=(N-D)/(A-D)\ge1$ and $S\ge0$. It is at least
$Dc_0^2/(128\varepsilon_*^2)$. The declared joint-degree bound is
\[
 J_{\rm reg}=H(2uv-v^2)+2(1+\tau H)F
 \ge\frac{D^2HBb}{4}
 >\frac{D^2c_0^3}{150994944\varepsilon_*^4}.
\]
For every permitted incidence threshold $D+1\le L\le A$, its multiplier
in the current regular MCA bound is
\[
 \frac{N-L+1}{A-L+1}\lambda_{\rm agr}\ge1.
\]
The other terms are nonnegative, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Graded nonmonomial row sources]\label{cor:graded-nonmonomial-cost}
The Eq63 conclusion also holds for a finite-dimensional total-jet-degree-graded
space $W=\bigoplus_q W_q\subseteq\mathbb F[Y_0,Y_1]$, stable under
$Y_0$-translation, with the full global source
\[
 \mathbb F[X]W\cap\{\operatorname{wt}_{(1,D,D-1)}<mA\}.
\]
Assume the same characteristic guard, and use its exact finite local rank
in each jet degree in the row test. No nonmonomial Eq64 assertion is made.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In each $W_q$, echelonize by increasing $Y_1$ exponent. A pivot vector is
$f_v=Y_0^{q-v}Y_1^v+\sum_{j>v}c_jY_0^{q-j}Y_1^j$, of derivative weight
$Dq-v$. Its pivot determines its full coefficient-prefix length
$mA-Dq+v$; the distinct pivots show that these prefixes form a basis of
the weighted-cutoff source. Under $Y_1\mapsto tY_1$, rescale by $t^{-v}$.
The pivot and its prefix length persist at $t=0$, so the limiting global
source is exactly the full-prefix monomial source of those pivots.
Extend the generic local action by $X\mapsto t^{-1}X$: it fixes
$E=Y_0-XY_1$ and preserves the contact ideal. Thus the finite local rank
is generically unchanged and cannot increase at the limit, separately
in each total degree. Source dimensions are preserved degreewise.
Translation stability survives the limit and gives a downward-$Y_0$
monomial support under the characteristic guard. Caps cannot increase.
Theorem~\ref{thm:current-graded} applies to the resulting positive row test.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of current-method tightness]
The mandatory triangle follows for every coordinatewise order ideal,
without assuming that rearrangement preserves a graded moment. Both tests cover supports downward only in $Y_0$: the row test uses
excess deletion and grouped positive sorting, while the column test uses
weighted height sorting. The preceding corollary covers a graded translation-stable nonmonomial
class for Eq63 only. Arbitrary filtered nonmonomial spaces and the
alternative equal-weight coefficient cutoff remain outside these statements. The characteristic hypothesis remains the stronger
local-rank hypothesis of this note, not merely the reconstruction
condition $p>\max\{D,b\}$. Optimizing actual component degrees, exploiting
global kernel dependencies, or using additional equations is outside the
numerical ledger considered here. When $A/N=a_0+\varepsilon$ and
$\varepsilon\ge1/N$, the corrected margin $\varepsilon_*$ is
$\Theta(\varepsilon)$ as $N$ grows; near rounding one must retain its
explicit formula. No intrinsic lower bound on lists or MCA exceptions
follows from this theorem.
\end{remark}
