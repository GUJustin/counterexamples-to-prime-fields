\subsection{An elementary low-rate boundary case}
\label{sec:vanishing-rate}

The fixed positive rate qualification matters in the remaining question
about separation proportional to $\eta$. If the rate may tend to zero,
even the constant code admits a line with exactly one far point.
This elementary boundary case is separate from our fixed-rate obstructions.

\begin{theorem}[A punctured nearby line for the constant code]
\label{fg:punctured-line}
For every finite $c_1,c_2\ge1$, there are a fixed integer $D\ge2$ and
a constant $a>1$ such that, for every sufficiently large prime $p$,
every dimension-one Reed--Solomon code of length
$n=\lfloor p^{1/a}\rfloor$ has the following properties, regardless
of its evaluation domain.
Put $\rho=1/n$, $\eta=D/n$, and $\theta=1-\rho-\eta$.
There is a nonconstant received line $f+zg$ for which
\[
 \dist(f+zg,C)\le\theta\quad\text{for every }z\ne0,
 \qquad \dist(f,C)=\theta+\frac\eta D.
\]
There is no correlated agreement at the threshold, $\theta$ is strictly
below Elias, and $c_1n2^{c_2H_2(\rho)/\eta}=o(p)$.
Here $n=o(p)$ and $p=\Theta(n^a)$, but $\rho\to0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose an integer $D>c_2$ and a real
$1+c_2/D<a<2$. Some monic degree-$D$ polynomial $f$ over $\F_p$
has $\Omega_D(p)$ distinct values with exactly $D$ preimages.
Indeed, associate each $D$-subset of $\F_p$ with its monic locator
$F$, and group the locators by $f=F-F(0)$. There are at most
$p^{D-1}$ groups, so one contains at least
$\binom pD/p^{D-1}=\Omega_D(p)$ locators.
Within a group, distinct locators give distinct full fibers of $f$.

Select $L=\lfloor n/(2D)\rfloor$ such fibers as the core, of size
$N=DL$, and take their $L$ values as constant codewords. On the core
put $g=0$; add $q=n-N$ distinct outside coordinates. At every outside
point $x$, the $L$ differences between the selected constants and
$f(x)$ are distinct and nonzero. Choose $g(x)$ independently and
uniformly in $\F_p^*$ on the new coordinates. Each fixed $z\ne0$
misses all the selected values at every new coordinate with probability
\[
 \left(1-\frac{L}{p-1}\right)^q.
\]
If it does not miss, one constant has its $D$ core agreements and
one new agreement. The expected number of uncovered nonzero parameters
is therefore at most
\[
 (p-1)\left(1-\frac L{p-1}\right)^q
 \le p\exp\!\left(-\frac{qL}{p-1}\right)<1,
\]
since $qL/(p-1)=\Omega_D(n^{2-a})$.
Some direction thus makes every nonzero parameter nearby at threshold
$A=D+1$. At zero, the degree of $f$ bounds the agreement with any
constant by $D$, attained on the selected fibers. This gives the exact
far-point distance and excludes correlated agreement at $D+1$.

The sufficient Elias inequality has logarithm
\[
 \log\!\left(\frac{p^D A^A(n-A)^{n-A}}{n^n}\right)
 =\bigl(aD-(D+1)\bigr)\log n+O(1)>0.
\]
The small-rate entropy expansion gives
$c_1n2^{c_2H_2(1/n)/\eta}=n^{1+c_2/D+o(1)}=o(p)$.
Finally, the constant code is unchanged by a coordinate permutation.
Relabeling the constructed words therefore transfers the statement
to any prescribed evaluation domain of the same size.
\end{proof}

For $c_1=c_2=1$, take $D=2$, $a=5/3$, and
$n=\lfloor p^{3/5}\rfloor$. An explicit core polynomial is
\[
 f(X)=X^2-(N+1)X,\qquad N=2\lfloor n/4\rfloor.
\]
The core $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ consists of $N/2$ full fibers,
namely the pairs $\{x,N+1-x\}$. The unique far point is $\eta/2$
beyond the radius, where $\eta=2/n$. The exact checker
\path{verify_constant_code_density.py} in
\path{research/fixed_gap_padding/} certifies all $p-1$ nonzero parameters
for $p=2^{61}-1$, $n=104159249330$, and for two larger primes.
It also verifies the fiber-count identity by exhaustive small-field
enumeration. The large-field results are existence certificates, not
enumerations of those enormous lines.

The constant code's actual maximum list size at this radius is exactly
$\lfloor n/(D+1)\rfloor$: distinct constant values use disjoint agreement
sets, and assigning blocks of $D+1$ positions attains the bound.
Thus its actual list size times $n$ is $\Theta(n^2)>p$ in this
construction. This is not an actual-list counterexample, nor does it
address the fixed positive-rate separation question.
