\paragraph{What a growing fixed-gap list would imply.}
The same averaging mechanism gives a useful transfer without requiring
the received word to have a low-degree polynomial representation.
The conclusion here concerns the entire agreement support of a nearby
candidate; it does not assert the absence of ordinary correlated agreement.

\begin{proposition}[List amplification with exact parameter scaling]
\label{prop:exact-list-amplification}
Let $k+1\le A\le N$, $k\ge2$, and let $p\ge2N+1$ be prime.
Suppose a word on $N$ distinct points of $\F_p$ has $L$ distinct
degree-$<k$ candidates, each with at least $A$ agreements.
There is a Reed--Solomon code of length $2N$ and dimension $k$ over
the same prime field, and a received line $f+zg$, with at least
\[
 J\ge \left\lceil\frac{pAL}{p+3AL}\right\rceil
       \ge \frac14\min\{AL,p\}
\]
distinct labels $z$ for which some nearby candidate has at least $A$
agreements and $g$ has no degree-$<k$ interpolant on its entire
agreement support. Thus source rate $\rho$ and gap $\eta$ become
exactly $\rho/2$ and $\eta/2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Some coordinate $a$ belongs to at least $\ell\ge AL/N$ candidate
agreement supports. Retain those candidates and form
\[
 Q_P(X)=\frac{P(X)-w(a)}{X-a},\qquad
 f(x)=\frac{w(x)-w(a)}{x-a},\quad g(x)=0\quad(x\ne a).
\]
The $Q_P$ are distinct of degree at most $k-2$, and have at least
$A-1\ge k$ agreements on the $N-1$ retained coordinates.
At each of the $R=p-N$ unused coordinates let $s_x$ be the number
of distinct values of the $Q_P$. Counting pairwise coincidences and
applying Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \frac1R\sum_xs_x\ge
 \mu:=\frac{\ell R}{R+(\ell-1)(k-2)}.
\]
Choose the $q=N+1$ unused coordinates with largest $s_x$, put $g=1$
there, and choose their $f$ values independently and uniformly.
The expected union of the label sets $\{Q_P(x)-f(x)\}$ is at least
$p(1-(1-\mu/p)^q)$, exactly as in Lemma~\ref{fg:averaged-padding}.
Every label in this union adds a new agreement to one selected candidate.
A degree-$<k$ interpolant for $g$ on that candidate's support would
vanish at its at least $k$ old agreements, hence vanish identically,
contradicting its value $1$ at the new agreement.

For the count, $R\ge p/2$ gives
\[
 \frac{q\mu}{p}\ge
 \frac{N\ell}{p+2(k-2)\ell}
 \ge\frac{N\ell}{p+2N\ell}
 \ge\frac{AL}{p+2AL}.
\]
Using $1-(1-u)^q\ge1-e^{-qu}\ge qu/(1+qu)$ proves the claimed
bound. The new length is $N-1+q=2N$, while its dimension and agreement
threshold remain $k$ and $A$, proving exact parameter scaling.
\end{proof}

At fixed positive rate and gap, this gives
$J=\Omega(\min\{NL,p\})$. In particular, a list lower bound $N^c$
over superpolynomial prime fields would force $\Omega(N^{c+1})$
full-support exceptions at half the rate and gap. A universal linear
full-support bound at the latter parameters would imply constant
source list sizes whenever $p/N\to\infty$. This identifies a sufficient
route to exponent lower bounds; it does not supply the growing lists.
The full-length lists with $N=p-1$ fall outside its field-size hypothesis.

\begin{corollary}[Amplification within an open agreement regime]
\label{cor:sparse-list-amplification}
Under the source hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-list-amplification},
fix rational $0<\tau\le1$ with $\tau N$ integral. There is a line of
length $(1+\tau)N$, dimension $k$, and agreement threshold $A$ with
at least
\[
 \frac{\tau}{5}\min\{AL,p\}
\]
full-support MCA-bad labels. Its rate and agreement fraction are
$\rho/(1+\tau)$ and $a/(1+\tau)$, where $\rho=k/N$ and $a=A/N$.
If $a>h(\rho)$ for a continuous sufficient agreement curve $h$, then
every sufficiently small positive $\tau$ preserves that strict inequality
at the output parameters. If $a^2<\rho$, the output also remains below
Johnson.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the same proof with $q=\tau N+1$ padding coordinates. Before
specializing $q$, its count gives
\[
 J\ge\frac{pq\ell}{p+(2(k-2)+q)\ell}
   \ge\frac{pqAL}{Np+(2(k-2)+q)AL}.
\]
Here the first inequality follows from
$\mu\ge\ell p/(p+2(k-2)\ell)$ and
$p(1-(1-\mu/p)^q)\ge pq\mu/(p+q\mu)$; the second uses monotonicity
in $\ell$. Since $q\ge\tau N$ and $2(k-2)+q<4N$, this is at least
$\tau pAL/(p+4AL)\ge(\tau/5)\min\{AL,p\}$.
The old $A-1\ge k$ agreements still exclude a full-support direction
witness. The parameter identities are exact. The strict inequality
$a/(1+\tau)>h(\rho/(1+\tau))$ follows by continuity at $\tau=0$.
Finally $a^2/(1+\tau)^2<\rho/(1+\tau)$ whenever $a^2<\rho$.
\end{proof}

Thus, within any such open regime, source lists of size $\Omega(N^c)$
over sufficiently large primes would force $\Omega(n^{c+1})$ full-support
exceptions at nearby parameters in the same regime. This remains a
conditional statement: it requires the growing source lists. For arbitrary
lengths, taking $q=\lfloor\tau N\rfloor+1$ preserves the strict margin
eventually, with parameters converging to the displayed pair.
