\subsubsection{Three simultaneous additions}
The same fresh-five family admits a triangle of fresh intersections.  We use
$Q_0,Q_2,Q_5$ from Table~\ref{tab:fresh-five-cubics}, in the same affine
coordinate as that table.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:eleven-cubic-padding}
Over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{39})$ there is a received word on nineteen distinct
coordinates whose complete nearest list among polynomials of degree at most
three consists of eleven polynomials, each agreeing at exactly seven
coordinates.  Such examples also exist over arbitrarily large prime fields.
A degree-thirteen pullback gives length $247$, dimension $40$, and agreement
$91$, with at least eleven polynomials in the threshold list; these parameters
lie strictly above the first-order proximity threshold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Retain the sixteen coordinates and received values of the rational eight-word
seed.  Add the two rational coordinates
\[
 r=-\frac{80}{163},\qquad s=-\frac{135}{262},
\]
with values $Q_0(r)=Q_2(r)$ and $Q_0(s)=Q_5(s)$, respectively.  Finally add
either root $\theta$ of
\[
 g(U)=U^2+\frac{4175}{3826}U+\frac{4525}{15304},
\]
with value $Q_2(\theta)=Q_5(\theta)$.  Its discriminant is
$117975/14638276=55^2\cdot39/3826^2$.  The three added coordinates are
pairwise distinct and avoid all sixteen old coordinates.  The relevant
polynomial differences vanish at the stated coordinates, so each of
$Q_0,Q_2,Q_5$ gains two matches and all eight original candidates retain their
seven matches.

Completeness is checked by a finite exact interpolation certificate, not by
counting only these eleven candidates.  Any cubic agreeing at seven of the
nineteen coordinates agrees at at least four old coordinates.  Those
coordinates and their received values are rational, so the cubic is uniquely
determined over $\mathbb Q$ by one of the $\binom{16}{4}=1820$ old four-subsets.
The resulting $1516$ distinct interpolants are evaluated at all old
coordinates, at $r,s$, and modulo the irreducible quadratic $g$ for the last
coordinate.  Exactly the eleven displayed candidates have seven matches;
none has eight.  The exact coefficients, supports, and exhaustive replay are
recorded in \path{research/relaxed_eight_cubic_route/eleven_cubic_certificate.py}
and its accompanying JSON certificate.

All distinctness conditions and failed interpolation equalities exclude only
finitely many primes.  Passing to primes that split completely in a normal
closure of the coefficient field therefore gives the claimed arbitrarily
large prime-field examples, including completeness of the source list.

For the pullback choose $c$ away from the nineteen coordinates and substitute
$U=T^{13}+c$.  All nineteen fibers then have thirteen distinct roots in a
number-field extension.  Pulling back the eleven cubics gives eleven
polynomials of degree at most $39$, with $91$ matches on $247$ coordinates.
After adjoining the fiber roots and again taking completely split good
primes, these are also prime-field examples.  Completeness is not asserted
for the pulled-back list.

For the threshold comparison put $\rho=40/247$, $a=91/247=7/19$,
$t^2=\rho/2$, and $K=3at^2+t^4-a^3$.  These parameters are in the low-rate
branch, and $a>t$, $K>0$, and
\[
 4t^6-K^2=\frac{4666871}{1343677407241}>0.
\]
Thus $a$ strictly exceeds the first-order threshold.  Exact tests of the
smaller positive pullback degrees show that thirteen is the first degree
with this property for this source.
\end{proof}
Within the eight fresh-five cubics, this padding is optimal for three added
coordinates: the exact factor census of all pair differences shows that no
fresh coordinate, including infinity, has three of these cubics taking the
same section value.  Each added coordinate can therefore supply at most two
matches to this family, and each promoted cubic requires two.  The triangle
attains the resulting upper bound of three promotions.  This is a statement
about this finite family, not an asymptotic list-size lower bound.
