\subsection{Proper denominators through cover degree nine}
\label{sec:small-cover-quarter-obstruction}

The same norm argument gives a finite extension of
Theorem~\ref{thm:seven-bank-one-pole-obstruction}.
Here the denominator degree is limited by the intended quarter-rate
construction; no assertion for arbitrary denominator degree is made.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:small-cover-quarter-obstruction}
Let a characteristic-zero seven-cubic bank and a degree-$e$ rational cover
$\psi=[R:S]$ satisfy the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:seven-bank-one-pole-obstruction}.  Suppose
\[
 2\le e\le9,\qquad 0\le h\le\lfloor e/2-1\rfloor.
\]
There is no proper witness $N/D$, with binary forms of degrees
$3e+h$ and $h$, respectively, having at least $7e$ agreements with
the pulled-back word $S^3w$, where $D$ has no zero on the selected
core and $\gcd(N,D)=1$.
For $h=0$, exclude the seven original pulled-back polynomials from
what is called a witness.
Zeros of $D$ may coincide with zeros of $S$ and may have multiplicity.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Extend constants algebraically.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:seven-bank-rigidity}, it suffices to treat the two
archived banks; its transformations preserve these assertions.
Put $v=N/(DS^3)$ and let $t$ be the degree of the map to the
normalization of the image of $(\psi,v)$.  The positive part of the
signed function
\[
 P\longmapsto\operatorname{pole}_P(v)
              -3\operatorname{pole}_P(\psi)
\]
is exactly the degree-$h$ zero divisor of $D$.  Pole orders, and hence
this positive part, pull back through the intermediate map.  Thus
$t\mid e$ and, for $h>0$, $t\mid h$.  By L\"uroth's theorem the
intermediate curve is rational.  Separability of the selected fibers
means a descended match accounts for exactly $t$ original matches.
The descended problem therefore has parameters $(e/t,h/t)$ and at
least $7e/t$ matches.

First take $h=0$.  Pairwise root counting against the seven old
polynomials shows that all $7e$ matched points must be the full
preimages of the seven triple buckets: if $T,Q$ count matched points
in triple and quadruple buckets, then
$T+Q=7e$ and $3T+4Q\le21e$.
Let $W$ interpolate the seven triple-bucket word values.  Each old cubic matches only three of those seven triple buckets.
Thus a cubic through all seven values would be an eighth distinct
cubic, contradicting the universal maximum-seven theorem.
Consequently $4\le d=\deg W\le6$.
The binary form
\[
 N S^{d-3}-W^{\mathrm{hom}}(R,S)
\]
has degree $de<7e$ and vanishes at those $7e$ distinct points, so it is
zero.  This would give $v=W(\psi)$, whose pole divisor has degree
$de>3e$, contrary to $(v)_\infty\le3\psi^*(\infty)$.

For $h=1$ use Theorem~\ref{thm:seven-bank-one-pole-obstruction}.
The only nontrivial intermediate-field cases still needed below are
$(e,h)=(6,2),(8,2),(9,3)$, descending respectively to $(3,1)$,
$(4,1)$, and $(3,1)$, all excluded by that theorem.
We may consequently assume the remaining maps $(\psi,v)$ are
birational.

Their primitive norm equation has the form
\[
 F(X,V)=\sum_{j=0}^{e} A_j(X)V^{e-j},
 \qquad \deg A_j\le3j+h.
\]
The pole argument above proves these bounds even with repeated poles
or poles over infinity.  Its irreducible closure on the third
Hirzebruch surface has arithmetic genus at most
\[
 g_{e,h}=(e-1)(3e/2+h-1).
\]
A fiber containing $m$ distinct matching points contributes at least
$\binom m2$ to its singularity delta.  Select $7e$ matches.  Pairwise
root bounds against the old degree-$(3e+h)$ polynomials give
\[
 T+Q=7e,\qquad3T+4Q\le7(3e+h),\qquad T\ge7(e-h).
\]
Convexity, using $e\ge2h+2$, therefore gives
\[
 \delta\ge7\left\{\binom{e-h}{2}+\binom h2\right\}.
\]
For $h=2$, this exceeds $g_{e,2}$ for every $e\ge7$;
at $e=7$ the comparison is $77>69$.
For $(e,h)=(9,3)$ it is $126>124$.
Only $(6,2)$ and $(8,3)$ remain.

For $(6,2)$, the delta budget $50$ forces the unique pattern with
four matches in each triple bucket and two in each quadruple bucket.
The norm flags give $84$ coefficients.  Vanishing of all Hasse
partials of total order below the fiber multiplicity gives a
$91\times84$ homogeneous matrix.  For both banks it has full column
rank: independently replayed maximal minors are nonzero modulo
$29$ and $83$, respectively.

For $(8,3)$ write triple multiplicities as $5+u_j$, quadruple
multiplicities as $3+v_j$, and $s=T-35$.  The genus budget $98$ gives
\[
 s\in\{0,1,2\},\quad
 \sum u_j=s,\quad\sum v_j=-s,\quad
 \sum(u_j^2+v_j^2)\le14-4s.
\]
The seven old-candidate deficits are nonnegative integers summing to
$s$.  For each such deficit vector and each bounded integer vector
$u$, invert the fixed seven-by-seven quadruple-incidence matrix to
recover $v$; retain precisely the integral vectors satisfying the
bounds.  This exhaustive rational-arithmetic enumeration gives
$386$ Paley patterns and $410$ orbit-$2$ patterns.  The norm flags
have $144$ coefficients.  Every corresponding Hasse-jet matrix has
rank $144$ over the same good residue field.  All $796$ selected
maximal minors were independently reconstructed and replayed.

For clarity, these are linear specialization certificates: the base
coordinates and word values are integral at the chosen good prime,
and each saved nonzero minor is the reduction of the same
characteristic-zero minor.  No modular nonlinear inconsistency
inference is involved.  The reproducible census, matrix construction,
and independent minor checks are recorded in the companion
\texttt{sextic\_two\_pole\_norm\_gate} and
\texttt{octic\_three\_pole\_route} directories.
The resulting trivial kernels exclude a nonzero primitive norm
relation in both remaining cases.
\end{proof}

Clearing $D$ gives degree at most $3e+h$ on $14e$ coordinates, so
$h\le\lfloor e/2-1\rfloor$ is exactly the condition
$3e+h+1\le14e/4$.  The corollary excludes this specific augmentation
mechanism through degree nine, not arbitrary eight-word constructions
at quarter rate.
