# Non-Fano orbits 0 and 1 are impossible in characteristic different from two

This proves exclusion over every field of characteristic different from two,
not merely for the rational-node specialization. We may extend constants to
an algebraic closure. The incidence conventions and representatives are those
in `design_orbits.jsonl`. All fourteen evaluation points are distinct and the
seven polynomial sections have degree at most three.

## The forced involution

For both representatives, the triple345 node is distinct from all seven
quadruple nodes b_0,...,b_6. Pair differences within345 have their other two
roots respectively at (b_2,b_5), (b_1,b_4), (b_0,b_3). Dividing the identity
(P3-P4)+(P4-P5)+(P5-P3)=0 by the linear form of the triple node shows that
these three quadratic root polynomials are linearly dependent, with all three
coefficients nonzero. The polynomials are pairwise coprime, since their six
roots are distinct.

They therefore span a basepoint-free pencil defining a degree-two map of
projective lines. In characteristic different from two this map is separable;
its deck involution has two distinct fixed points and exchanges the two roots
in each pair. Neither fixed point is among those six nodes. Normalize the
involution to X -> -X, choosing its point at infinity different from b_6.
This is always possible, since only one additional quadruple node is involved.
Thus all seven quadruple nodes are finite and can be written

    (b_0,...,b_6)=(u,v,z,-u,-v,-z,d).

Here u,v,z are nonzero, their squares are pairwise distinct, and d differs
from each of ±u,±v,±z. A triple node may be at infinity; all ensuing difference
identities are identities of homogeneous binary cubics and cover this case.

## Orbit 1

The pair-root quadratics associated to triple156 have root pairs
(-u,-v), (-z,d), (u,v). Their dependence forces -zd=uv: the first and last
span exactly the pencil generated by X^2+uv and X, and the middle monic
quadratic has constant term -zd. These first and last are independent because
u+v is nonzero and the characteristic is not two.

For triple257 the pairs are (u,v), (z,d), (-u,-v). The same argument forces
zd=uv. Hence 2uv=0, a contradiction. This proof uses no interpolation or
symbolic computation.

## Orbit 0: three necessary linear equations

Interpolate the seven quadruple received values by W of degree at most six.
For candidate i let L_i be the quartic with roots at its four quadruple
incidences. Necessarily P_i=W mod L_i. Subtracting the common cubic part
normalizes W=a X^4+b X^5+c X^6. At least one amplitude is nonzero.
Put alpha=a+(u^2+v^2+z^2)c; use amplitude vector (alpha,b,c).

The necessary triple conditions124,156,257 give the three rows below, each
with dot product zero against this amplitude vector:

    (-d^2 v-u z(u+v+z),
      d v(-d^2+u^2+uv+uz+vz+z^2),
     -v(d^2-u^2)(d^2-z^2)),

    (-dz-uv,
      z(-d^2+du+v^2+vz),
     -z(d-u)(d^2-v^2)),

    ( dz-uv,
      z(d^2+du-v^2-vz),
      z(d+u)(d^2-v^2)).

Their determinant is

    2 u^2 v z (v-d)(z-d)(v+d)(z+d)(v+z)^2,

which is nonzero under the distinct-node guards. This contradicts the
nonzero amplitude vector.

For completeness, the row extraction does not presume finite triple nodes
or discard arbitrary resultant factors. For a triple with pair-quad root
counts (1,2,2), write its differences A=P_i-P_j and B=P_i-P_k. Let a0 be
the single quad root of A; let V0 and W0 be the monic quadratics of the two
quad roots of B and B-A. Dividing by the common triple-node linear form
forces the quadratic pair to be proportional to

    U=W0(a0)V0-V0(a0)W0,    V=W0(a0)V0.

Consequently A V-B U=0. Evaluations W0(a0),V0(a0) are nonzero. This applies
to124. For a triple sharing one quadruple node h, divide A,B by X-h first.
Their remaining prescribed quad roots are a0,b0,c0 for A,B,B-A. After
removing the additional triple root, the linear pair is proportional to

    U=(c0-b0)(X-a0),    V=(c0-a0)(X-b0).

Again A V-B U=0; this applies to156 and257. Taking the leading coefficient
of these identities and dividing only the nonzero scalar2 yields the displayed
rows. A common triple root at infinity simply makes the common linear factor
constant in the affine chart, so the same identities remain valid.
