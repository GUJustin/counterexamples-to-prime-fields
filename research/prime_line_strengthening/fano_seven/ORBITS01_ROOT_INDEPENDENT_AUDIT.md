# Independent root audit of the orbit 0 and orbit 1 exclusions

Both exclusions pass in characteristic different from two, over an algebraic closure. This is a global check, not a fixed-node numerical test.

## Common normalization

In both designs, the candidate triple 345 has a common triple-node. Each of its three pair differences also has two prescribed quadruple-nodes, and these three pairs partition six distinct nodes. Dividing the cubic differences by the common triple-node factor gives a nontrivial constant dependence between the three binary quadratic root polynomials. They span a basepoint-free pencil, hence define a separable degree-two map in odd characteristic. Its deck involution exchanges each prescribed pair. After a projective change the involution is X -> -X, so the ordered quadruple-node list is

    (u,v,z,-u,-v,-z,d).

The six paired nodes are not branch points. Choose the fixed point sent to infinity to differ from the seventh node d; one of the two fixed points always does. Thus all seven quadruple-nodes are finite, u,v,z are nonzero and pairwise distinct up to sign, and d avoids them. A triple-node may be infinity; the identities below remain valid for binary forms.

All seven received values at the quadruple-nodes have a unique interpolant of degree at most six. Modulo adding one common cubic to all candidates, write it as a X^4 + b X^5 + c X^6. Every candidate is its remainder modulo the quartic locator of its four quadruple matches. This gives every possible candidate family, not merely a convenient subclass.

## Orbit 0

I independently checked the two cross-product identities used by `nonfano_involution.py`.

For a triple whose pairwise quadruple-root counts are (1,2,2), let A=P_i-P_j have its single prescribed quadruple-root alpha, and let B=P_i-P_k and B-A=P_j-P_k have quadratic locators V and W. After dividing their common triple-node factor, the latter two are scalar multiples of V,W. Evaluation at alpha forces their scalar ratio. Hence

    A*[W(alpha)V] - B*[W(alpha)V - V(alpha)W] = 0.

For a triple sharing one quadruple-node, first divide that common factor. The three remaining prescribed quadruple-roots alpha,beta,gamma similarly force

    A*[(gamma-alpha)(X-beta)] - B*[(gamma-beta)(X-alpha)] = 0.

These identities also hold when the new triple-node is infinity. Taking one coefficient from each identity is a necessary linear condition on (a,b,c).

I reviewed the exact script and its JSON. Each removed row content is only 2, so no unproved geometric factor was divided out. The rows for triples 124,156,257 have determinant

    2 u^2 v z (v-d)(z-d)(v+d)(z+d)(v+z)^2.

Every factor is nonzero under the normalization guards. Thus a=b=c=0, making all candidates equal after restoring their common cubic. This is a contradiction. The displayed determinant is an exact symbolic identity, not a sampled numerical rank.

## Orbit 1

No amplitude computation is needed. Triple 156 forces dependence of quadratics with root pairs

    (-u,-v), (-z,d), (u,v).

The first and third span the pencil generated by X^2+uv and X, because characteristic is odd and u+v is nonzero. Membership of the middle monic quadratic forces -zd=uv.

Triple 257 instead has root pairs

    (u,v), (z,d), (-u,-v),

and forces zd=uv. Together these imply 2uv=0, impossible. All roots in these two pencils are quadruple-nodes already known to be finite and distinct.
