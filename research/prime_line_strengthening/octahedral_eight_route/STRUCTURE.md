# Octahedral eight-section route

No earlier octahedral/S4 construction was found in the research notes searched.

Use the octahedral action generated by x -> i*x and x -> (x+1)/(x-1). On binary octics use the honest PGL2 action det(M)^(-4) P(M(U,V)); scalar representatives have no effect. The invariant octic is F8=U^8+14U^4 V^4+V^8. Its zero orbit has size8. The order-two special orbit of size12 is the zero set of F12=U^12-33U^8V^4-33U^4V^8+V^12. Take the domain to be that orbit plus a generic orbit of size24.

## Untwisted route is too weak

An actual orbit of eight sections requires a stabilizer C3 that fixes the section, not merely its projective line. An eigensection with a nontrivial C3 character generally gives24 actual sections. The C3-fixed octic space has dimension3.

An untwisted equivariant word is a scalar multiple of F8 on each domain orbit. C3 acts freely on the24- and12-point orbits, so matches on either occur in multiples of3. A noninvariant octic P differs nontrivially from each such scalar multiple of F8, and has at most8 zeros. Hence at most6 matches per orbit, total12. This cannot yield the target18/36.

## Sign twist restores a viable finite gate

Twist the section action by the sign character S4 -> {+1,-1}. Its restriction to C3 is trivial, so the candidate fixed space remains3-dimensional. A sign-equivariant word vanishes on the special12 orbit: its order-two stabilizer is an odd permutation, whereas the degree-eight fiber character there is +1. On the generic24 orbit it can be chosen as sign(g)*a*F8(g*x), with a nonzero amplitude.

For a candidate P fixed by C3, the three match classes (special zeros, positive generic values, negative generic values) each have at most6 points. Thus agreement18 requires exactly6 in all three.

The special12 orbit consists of four C3-orbits of size3. Choosing the two matched orbits gives only six possible candidates up to scaling. Indeed every C3-fixed octic vanishes at its two fixed points (the fiber character there is nontrivial); together with the six selected special zeros this exhausts degree8. The resulting product is C3-fixed under the degree-eight linearization.

For each of these six candidates let f=P/F8. There are eight left C3 cosets in S4, four even and four odd. Normalize a positive match to the identity coset by changing the generic-orbit representative. To obtain18 matches it is necessary and sufficient, subject to distinctness and exact-stabilizer guards, that one of the other three even cosets and two of the four odd cosets satisfy

    f(g*x)=sign(g)*f(x).

This gives18 triples of univariate polynomial equations per candidate,108 finite gcd tests in all. Numerators have degree at most16. Exclude roots in the special6-,8-,12-point orbits and zeros/poles of f. A surviving algebraic root gives a characteristic-zero generic orbit, after exact incidence and eight-distinct-section verification. No parameter grid is required.

A bounded exact pilot over Q(i,sqrt(2)) can build the six products from the orbit of1+sqrt(2), use the order-three generator x -> (i*x+1)/(1-i*x), and compute these gcds. A modular pilot alone would not establish a characteristic-zero witness; any positive must be lifted or recomputed exactly.
