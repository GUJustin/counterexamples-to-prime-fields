# Independent audit of Reynolds modal experiment

The modal optimization lemma is correct. Let H=mu_r, E=mu_(4r), and bank P_a(Y)=V(Y/a) for a in H. For each of the four cosets C of H, let M_C be the largest frequency of a value of V on C. Then

`max_words w min_(a in H) |{y in E:P_a(y)=w(y)}| = Σ_C M_C`.

For the upper bound, fix a coordinate y in C. As a ranges over H, y/a traverses C once, so at most M_C bank members match any chosen w(y). Summing over y gives total bank agreement at most r Σ_C M_C, and the minimum of the r agreements is at most their average. For the lower bound, choose a modal value separately on each coset and make w constant there. Every rotation then attains exactly M_C matches on C. Ties and coinciding modal values across cosets do not affect the lemma.

The lemma concerns matching **all r prescribed candidates**. It does not optimize a smaller subbank, establish their nearest-codeword status, or by itself construct bad affine-line labels. Distinct modal values across all four cosets are a separate useful property: without it, a constant polynomial may match several whole cosets, obstructing the intended nearest-orbit argument.

## Code audit

`root=generator(p)^d` has exact order 4r because p−1=4dr and generator(p) is primitive. Hence H is generated by root^4, and `t % 4` is exactly the coset label for root^t. The weaker subsequent assertions are sufficient sanity checks here because exact order already follows from the generator construction.

With e=2dr+1<p, factorials through e are invertible. The coefficient at Y^h is exactly binom(e,2dh+1) for 0<=h<r. The removed h=r coefficient is 1, corresponding to subtraction of X^(dr). Thus the code computes the claimed degree-(r−1) Reynolds polynomial and the original target `(1+Y^(2r))/2−Y^r` correctly.

The independent script `audit_reynolds_modal.py` uses integer binomial coefficients, exhaustive root sets rather than a primitive-root traversal, and direct evaluation. For five fixtures (p,r,d)=(37,3,3),(41,5,2),(61,3,5),(89,11,2),(101,5,5), it verifies the Reynolds average on every nonzero original coordinate, the root-of-unity filter on every square original coordinate, the agreement of every rotation with the modal word, five arbitrary-word double counts, and equality with the stored survey results. All checks pass under the watchdog (0.55 seconds, 7.3 MiB).

## Scope of the observed negative result

The stored survey has 377 prime (r,d,p) cases in its explicitly listed range. None has surplus over r for the original target word; the largest original surplus is −1. Modal optimization produces surplus in 44 cases, all at r=3. Thus no tested r>=5 has a word simultaneously giving all r bank members more than r agreements.

This is a bounded experimental statement. It does not exclude larger primes, different coefficient banks, or a proper subbank, and is not an asymptotic obstruction theorem.
