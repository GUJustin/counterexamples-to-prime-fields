## One precise symbolic identity still missing

The most concrete surviving target among these three is an OFF-POLE COMMON-REMAINDER identity for this already explicit shared-pole bank. For subsets B_j of its candidate indices, seek squarefree pairwise-coprime polynomials G_j, coprime to the pole locator, and polynomials R_j of degree below deg G_j, such that

    Q_S(X) ≡ R_j(X) mod G_j(X)   for every S in B_j.

Equivalently, G_j divides gcd_(S in B_j)(Q_S−Q_(S_j)). This is an exact algebraic identity, not a request to optimize a received word numerically. At the distinct roots of G_j it supplies one received value shared by every member of B_j. Across j, the necessary growth/coverage ledger is

    total offpole nodes = sum_j deg G_j = Theta(M),
    each retained candidate belongs to blocks of total degree Theta(M),
    weighted total incidences = sum_j |B_j| deg G_j = Theta(M²).

At n~4M,K=M+1, the archived exact pole count sharpens the target: offpole blocks must provide at least [2a0−1/8]M²+o(M²) incidences, where a0=(3+sqrt(133))/31. On ~3.5M offpole nodes the average bucket must have at least 0.23216M members, about46.43% of the bank. The coverage must also be balanced across candidates; the average alone is insufficient.

Necessary pair-degree checks must accompany any proposed identity: for distinct S,T,

    sum_(j:S,T in B_j) deg G_j <= deg(Q_S−Q_T) <=M,

after also accounting for already shared pole roots. Thus merely asserting a large common factor for arbitrary Paley masks is not justified. No such offpole factor identity or balanced family is currently proved in the checked artifacts. The existing convolution identity alone does not imply it.

This is a sharper symbolic task than another torsion-point census: either exhibit the congruences with a varying ell and the stated degree/coverage ledger, or derive a structural failure for the proposed block masks. A single small gcd or finite hit would still not establish scalable progress. No computation is proposed without an explicit proposed factor or group-theoretic formula.
